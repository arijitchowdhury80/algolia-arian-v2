# How B2B SaaS AEs Actually Prepare for a Call — and What That Means for the PRISM 60-Second Brief

**Date:** 2026-06-27
**Purpose:** Ground the PRISM AE hero surface (the "60-second pre-call brief") in the real prep workflow it replaces. This is a web-research proxy. The highest-signal version is primary interviews with real Algolia AEs — flagged explicitly in the final section.

**Plain-English bottom line:** AEs are *supposed* to spend 15–30 minutes prepping by stitching together 4–5 tools (CRM, LinkedIn, the prospect's site, news, old notes). In practice most do far less — they wing it or skim — because the stitching is tedious, repetitive, and never gets saved. The thing PRISM removes is not "research"; it's the **manual synthesis across tabs** an AE does in the last few minutes before a call. The brief wins or dies on three things: it must be **auto-ready** (not summoned), **scannable in one screen** (not a data dump), and **deliver value in the first session** or it gets abandoned like every other tool in the stack.

---

## Current workflow

The recommended prep is 10–15 min for SMB calls, 20–30 min for enterprise (HubSpot, Avoma, Salesmotion). The *actual* behavior is messier and shorter.

**What an AE opens, roughly in order** (Salesmotion, Streak, Avoma, Bravado):
1. **CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot)** — past activity, owner, stage, prior notes, who's talked to them.
2. **LinkedIn / Sales Navigator** — the person's role, tenure, background, recent posts (2–3 min).
3. **The prospect's website** — what they do, a recent press release, products.
4. **Google / news** — funding, exec changes, expansions in the last ~90 days (the "trigger event").
5. **Old notes / call transcript (Gong/Avoma)** — to recover a credible point of view from the last touch.
6. **Enrichment tools (ZoomInfo / 6sense / Apollo)** — firmographics and intent, when available.

Time reality: blogs cite a baseline "3–5 minutes" of research that they call insufficient; SDR-level prep is cited at ~20 min/call (MarketBetter). At the enterprise end, one 15-person team logged **1–2 hours per first call** hunting through websites, 10-Ks and investor decks (Salesmotion). The honest read across sources: **reps know they should prep, and many under-prep or skim** because the full ritual is expensive and boring. There is no single "do they prep or wing it" stat — but every source frames manual prep as the thing reps cut when busy.

**The deeper failure isn't finding info — it's that nothing is retained.** Reps "research the same account multiple times because they do not retain or centralize their findings — they check LinkedIn before a call, read a news article before an email, and Google the company before a QBR, never connecting the dots between sessions" (Salesmotion). Synthesis is redone from scratch every time.

> **What PRISM removes:** the 4–5-tab manual stitch-and-synthesize, repeated per call and never saved.

## Friction points

- **Context-switching tax.** "Research lives across LinkedIn, CRM, email, call notes, news alerts, enrichment data, and internal docs, so reps spend as much time switching contexts as they do finding insight" (Salesmotion). Toggling 4–5 tools to assemble one picture.
- **Synthesis, not search, is the expensive part.** "Finding a funding announcement or executive hire does not create value by itself" — the rep still has to turn raw facts into a point of view (Salesmotion). This is the cognitive work, and it's what gets skipped under time pressure.
- **No memory between sessions.** Same account re-researched for the call, the email, and the QBR (Salesmotion).
- **Copy-paste between tools.** Reps copy names, titles, news snippets and notes from CRM/LinkedIn/news into a scratch doc or the CRM note to assemble a brief. This manual assembly is the literal motion PRISM should eliminate.
- **Tool overload.** B2B sales teams use ~10 tools; 66% of reps say they feel overwhelmed by too many tools (Salesmotion). Prep adds yet another set of logins.
- **Cost framing (illustrative, not a benchmark):** Salesmotion models ~$345k/yr/rep of research time at scale — explicitly a scenario, but it captures why orgs care.

## 60-second info hierarchy (ranked)

If an AE has only 60 seconds, the goal is **credibility in the opening + a relevant question to ask** — not exhaustive knowledge. Gong's data anchors this: the best discovery calls ask **11–14 targeted questions** and uncover **3–4 business problems**, with a **43% talk / 57% listen** ratio. So the brief's job is to make the AE sound informed in the first 30 seconds and arm them with the *right* questions — not to brief them on everything.

Synthesizing Streak, Avoma, MarketBetter, Brief My Meeting and Gong, ranked by what an AE most needs to be credible:

1. **Who's on the call + their role and tenure.** Role tenure is a strong signal: someone <6 months in is reshaping the vendor stack (a buyer); 5 years in has entrenched relationships (status quo to dislodge). Highest-value single fact.
2. **The trigger / recent news (last ~90 days).** Funding, exec hires, launches, expansion, earnings commentary — the hook for a specific, non-generic opening line. This is the "why now."
3. **Company one-liner: what they do, size, stage.** Enough to not sound ignorant; not a profile dump.
4. **Top ~3 likely priorities / pain points.** Drives the discovery questions; maps to Gong's "uncover 3–4 problems."
5. **Relationship history with us.** Prior touches, open opps, stage, last interaction, objections raised — so the AE doesn't repeat a colleague or re-litigate a known objection.
6. **A suggested opening line + 2–3 discovery questions.** The output of all of the above — what the AE actually says.
7. **Anticipated objection(s).** Useful but lower priority for the first 60 seconds; matters more mid-call.

**Design read:** items 1–3 are the "don't embarrass yourself" layer (instant credibility); items 4–6 are the "win the call" layer (the question arsenal). The brief should physically separate these — credibility facts at a glance up top, the say-this/ask-this strategy block below.

## Entry model evidence

Three timing patterns appear in the sources (MarketBetter is most explicit, naming all three):
- **Calendar-triggered** — system watches for calls in the next ~60 min and prepares automatically.
- **Morning batch** — all briefs for the day generated before work starts.
- **On-demand / 2-minutes-before** — rep summons a brief right before dialing.

**The adoption data decisively favors auto-ready over summon.** "Proactive, signal-driven alerts get 3 to 5× higher usage than tools that require reps to log in and search" and "tools that require reps to pull information get abandoned, while tools that push relevant insights get used daily" (Salesmotion). The real-world prep pattern is the morning skim plus a last-minute glance — which matches *batch generation + a calendar-anchored surface that's already populated* when the AE looks.

**Design read:** PRISM should **not** rely on the AE remembering to open it and run a search. The brief should be **pre-generated and waiting** — tied to the calendar meeting, ready in the morning batch, and re-surfaced ~30–60 min before the call. Summon-on-demand should exist as a fallback, never the primary path.

## Adoption risks

Why pre-call tools get abandoned (Salesmotion ×2, Prospeo, HeyDAN, behavioral-design sources):

1. **Not in the workflow.** If the AE has to leave Salesforce/HubSpot to get the brief, the extra click is a wall. Native integration shows ~94% deployment success vs ~72% for weak integration. Poor integration is the #2 cause of abandonment (behind low perceived ROI).
2. **Information overload / no prioritization.** "Aggregation without prioritization creates information overload, which is functionally the same as having no tool at all." A dense profile is as useless as nothing.
3. **Any added friction loses.** Reps already spend ~72% of their week not selling; anything that adds friction loses to the path of least resistance.
4. **Slow time-to-value.** ~70% of new users abandon a product within 3 months without quick value; reps form opinions in the first week. Tools delivering value <14 days retain 92% at one year vs 67% for >30 days.
5. **Pull-based design.** Login-and-search tools die; push/proactive tools survive (the 3–5× usage gap above).

**Design read for PRISM:** auto-generated (no search), one-screen prioritized brief (no dump), embedded where the AE already is (CRM / calendar / Slack, not a separate destination), credible value on the very first call. Miss any one and adoption collapses regardless of data quality.

## What only primary AE interviews can confirm

The web proxy is strong on *patterns* but cannot confirm the *specifics of the Algolia AE motion*. Primary interviews should pin down:

1. **Do Algolia AEs actually prep, and for how long?** Real distribution (skim / 5 min / 30 min), not blog ideals. Is winging-it common?
2. **The real tool order and the actual CRM.** Salesforce vs Gong vs LinkedIn vs 6sense vs internal enablement — which they open first, and which they'd never leave.
3. **Where the copy-paste really happens.** What do they assemble by hand today, and into what (CRM note, Slack, scratch doc, deck)?
4. **What "credible in 60 seconds" means for a search/discovery pitch specifically** — e.g., does current search vendor, site search behavior, or a tech signal outrank generic news for *this* product?
5. **Entry trigger truth.** Calendar-anchored, morning batch, or 2-min-before — and on which device (desktop pre-call vs phone walking into a meeting)?
6. **The abandonment graveyard.** Which prep/intel tools Algolia AEs already abandoned and exactly why — the most direct guide to what PRISM must not repeat.
7. **Where the brief must live** to be opened every time — inside Salesforce, a Chrome extension, Slack, calendar, or standalone.

---

## Implications for the PRISM AE UI

1. **Auto-ready, not summoned.** Generate the brief on a calendar/morning-batch trigger; surface it pre-populated ~30–60 min before the call. Search-and-pull is the documented abandonment path. *(Entry model evidence)*
2. **One screen, two tiers.** Top tier = credibility-at-a-glance (who + role/tenure, the trigger/news, company one-liner). Lower tier = the strategy block (3 priorities, opening line, 2–3 discovery questions, relationship history). Built directly from the ranked hierarchy. *(60-second hierarchy)*
3. **Prioritize, never dump.** A dense profile equals no tool. Hard-rank and truncate; link to depth rather than show it. *(Adoption risk #2)*
4. **Embed where the AE already lives.** Salesforce/calendar/Slack surface beats a standalone destination; minimize clicks-to-brief. *(Adoption risk #1)*
5. **Synthesis is the product, not aggregation.** Don't show a funding headline — show "new VP Eng (3 mo) + recent funding → likely re-evaluating search stack; open with X, ask Y." That's the manual work PRISM removes. *(Friction)*
6. **First-call value or it's dead.** The very first brief must visibly save the prep and make the AE sound smart. <14-day value window. *(Adoption risk #4)*
7. **Memory across touches.** Persist the synthesized view so the same account isn't re-researched for call, email, and QBR. A differentiator against the "re-research every time" pain. *(Friction)*
