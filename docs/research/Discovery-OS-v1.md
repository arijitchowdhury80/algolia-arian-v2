# 1. Executive Summary

**10-second version:** The old discovery playbook — ask questions, fill MEDDPICC — is obsolete because the buyer now arrives having researched Algolia in ChatGPT before the call. Discovery OS replaces the question-led opening with **Exchange**: the rep enters with PRISM-generated, evidence-backed hypotheses about the buyer's business, trades those hypotheses for correction and deeper disclosure, and earns MEDDPICC fields through reciprocal dialog rather than extracting them through interrogation. MEDDPICC stays. The conversational engine that fills it changes.

## The thesis

In 2022, information asymmetry favored the seller. The rep knew more about Algolia's capability than the buyer did, and the buyer knew more about their own business than the rep did. Discovery was the mechanism for closing both halves.

In 2026, both halves have collapsed. 89–94% of B2B buyers now research with LLMs before the first call 🟢 (Forrester 2024 State of Business Buying; 6sense 2025, n≈4,000). 95% of winning vendors are on the Day One shortlist 🟢 (6sense 2025). 85% of buyers have established purchase requirements before first contact 🟢 (6sense 2024–2025). A rep opening with "tell me about your business" in 2026 signals that they are less prepared than the LLM the buyer already consulted.

Simultaneously, PRISM now delivers to every Algolia rep, before the first call: a site audit with 10-dimension scoring and live query screenshots, exec earnings-call quotes with Said-vs-Found analysis, tech stack inference, financial signals, trigger-event detection, competitive landscape, and buying-committee inference. What used to take three discovery calls to surface is available before the first one.

This creates an opportunity: the first call no longer needs to be about learning what is true. It can be about **confirming what is true in the buyer's own words, earning the right to ask what PRISM cannot see, and demonstrating sensemaking judgment the LLM cannot provide.**

## The paradigm: Exchange

Exchange is a discovery mechanism in which the rep enters the first call having already performed the diagnostic work a traditional discovery call would produce, then trades that pre-formed, falsifiable interpretation in return for correction, texture, and privileged information unavailable externally. The opening posture is: *"Here is what I think is true about your business — tell me where I'm wrong."*

The instrument of Exchange is the **Calibrated Hypothesis**: specific enough to prove the rep did real work, tentative enough to invite disconfirmation.

## The six principles

1. **Permissioned insight.** Insight lands better when the rep asks consent before sharing and invites correction.
2. **Calibrated hypothesis.** Offer interpretation, not conclusion. Lowers defensiveness. Increases corrective disclosure.
3. **Reciprocal value before request.** The rep contributes first, then asks. Reciprocity is the currency.
4. **Cost-of-inaction before ROI.** Executive buyers penalize premature ROI talk; they respond to consequence sizing.
5. **Diagnosis before recommendation.** Top-performing discovery calls limit early solutioning.
6. **Reaction-driven branching.** The buyer's reaction — not the rep's question list — determines the next move.

## The core loop

**Prep → Open → Hypothesize → Branch → Deepen → Commit → Handoff**

Each stage has specific moves, specific time-in-call expectations, specific sequencing rules, and specific PRISM output categories that drive it. Full treatment in Section 3.

## What Discovery OS replaces

- Front-loaded question banks used as the opening conversational engine of the first call
- "Tell me about your business" as the literal first move
- BANT-style qualification in the first ten minutes
- Deck-led first calls
- ROI promises in the opening half
- Defensive rebuttal of competitive narratives
- Single-threading on call one

## What Discovery OS preserves and augments

- **MEDDPICC as deal-state model and review lens.** Discovery OS is the *engine* that fills MEDDPICC fields through natural reciprocal dialog — not a replacement.
- **Persona awareness.** Merch / Product / Engineering / Executive speak differently. The methodology core is universal; persona is the vocabulary layer.
- **Implication logic from SPIN, gap logic from Gap Selling, commercial teaching from Challenger, value-articulation from Command of the Message.** These augment the methodology at specific stages of the loop.
- **Mutual Action Plan discipline.** Unchanged. Strong as it is.

## Why now, specifically

Three converging forces make Exchange the right answer in 2026 and make it unavailable before:

1. **PRISM.** No methodology can be insight-led if there is no pre-call insight to trade. PRISM is the currency source.
2. **AI-era buyer behavior.** Buyers now weight credibility over likability (82%, Sales Growth Company 2025 🟡), punish generic openings (73% avoid reps with irrelevant outreach, Gartner 2025 🟢), and reward reps who help them navigate conflicting AI-surfaced information (2.8× higher purchase ease from sensemaking suppliers 🟢, Gartner).
3. **The Algolia-specific competitive landmine.** The "Algolia is a developer tool / Constructor is for business users" narrative is pre-planted in most enterprise calls. It cannot be neutralized through traditional discovery. It must be pre-empted in the first five minutes via named moves grounded in buyer-specific data. PRISM produces the data; Exchange produces the moves.

## Required conditions for success

Exchange is more demanding than interrogation. It fails under specific, nameable conditions, and Algolia must address each explicitly:

1. **PRISM data quality must be rigorously QA'd** before any hypothesis is delivered to a customer. A confidently-framed wrong hypothesis in the first five minutes is nearly unrecoverable.
2. **Reps must have enough category depth to hold hypotheses loosely** and recover gracefully when corrected. Exchange is intolerant of both shallow preparation and overconfident assertion.
3. **Enablement must coach to invitation-to-correct behavior**, not just hypothesis delivery. Reps under time pressure will cut the invitation — and that collapses Exchange into a data-dressed pitch.
4. **Exceptions must be named and taught.** Exchange should not be deployed with hostile buyers, procurement-led regulated deals, or when PRISM confidence is low. Question-led discovery remains the right tool for those cases.
5. **Algolia must instrument A/B measurement** of Exchange vs. control openings within its own funnel to move the methodology from 🔵 inference to 🟢 proven.

# 2. The Paradigm: Exchange

**10-second version:** Exchange is structured reciprocal disclosure. The rep offers a calibrated hypothesis grounded in pre-call data. The buyer corrects or confirms. Every correction deepens the diagnosis. Every confirmation fills a MEDDPICC field with language the buyer owns. Trust is the byproduct, not the objective. Qualification happens naturally as a consequence, not as the primary goal.

## 2.1 What Exchange is

Exchange is a discovery mechanism grounded in five converging disciplines:

**Reciprocity and asymmetric disclosure** 🟢. Cialdini (1984) on reciprocity norms; Fehr, Kirchsteiger & Riedl (1993/1998) gift-exchange experiments (buyers paid above reservation prices when sellers signaled costly effort); Collins & Miller (1994) meta-analysis (k=94) demonstrating that disclosure lifts both liking and reciprocal disclosure. The research consistently shows that information volunteered before being requested triggers stronger reciprocation than information extracted through direct questioning.

**McKinsey/Bain first-meeting protocol** 🟡. Minto (1987) *Pyramid Principle* — answer first, justify second. Rasiel (1999) *The McKinsey Way* — hypothesis-driven problem solving. Enns (2010) *The Firm* — peer-to-peer fit assessment as the opening posture. Khalsa & Illig (2008) — "diagnose before prescribe." The consulting tradition explicitly trains senior partners to open client conversations with a hypothesis, framed tentatively, and to treat the conversation as a joint diagnostic exercise, not a discovery interview.

**Clinical diagnostic interviewing** 🟢. Baile et al. (2000) SPIKES protocol for delivering difficult medical information — notably: ask permission before delivering findings, invite the patient to share their own interpretation, offer findings tentatively. Miller & Rollnick (2013) motivational interviewing — evocation (drawing the problem statement from the patient), rolling with resistance (not arguing), and the "righting-reflex problem" (telling someone what's wrong with them triggers defense of the status quo).

**Non-coercive elicitation** 🟢. Alison et al. (2013) ORBIT model (n=418 forensic interviews; structural equation modeling) — empathy, autonomy-granting, and adaptive behavior significantly predict interview yield. Voss (2016) tactical empathy and accusation audit. Dreeke (2017) *Code of Trust* — suspend ego, honor reason. All converge on: the person being interviewed cooperates more when they feel respected, when the interviewer demonstrates prior knowledge without claiming certainty, and when correction is invited rather than challenged.

**Expert advisory practice** 🟡. Executive coaching intake protocols (ICF standards). Private banking first-meeting norms. Medical second-opinion consultations. All share a common structural feature: the advisor opens with evidence of preparation, offers a tentative formulation of the client's situation, and invites the client to correct and extend.

## 2.2 What Exchange is not

Exchange is frequently misunderstood. Name the anti-patterns explicitly:

**Exchange is not Challenger.** Challenger (Dixon & Adamson 2011 🟡) seeks a "never-thought-of-it-that-way" moment driven by seller-superior insight. Exchange seeks a *correction* — the buyer teaching back. Challenger optimizes for constructive tension and rep control. Exchange optimizes for buyer elaboration. Both can produce reframes, but the directionality is inverted. If the rep leaves an Exchange call feeling "I taught them something," the rep missed the point.

**Exchange is not a pitch with data dressing.** If the rep narrates conclusions rather than offering them for falsification, Exchange collapses into show-and-tell and triggers the righting-reflex. The tentativeness token is structural, not stylistic — it is what makes Exchange work.

**Exchange is not SPIN.** SPIN (Rackham 1988 🟢, n=35,000) sequences Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff *questions*. Exchange inverts the opening: the rep pre-states the implication as hypothesis and asks the buyer to confirm or amend. SPIN is the opening fallback when Exchange fails (see §4.3 escalation).

**Exchange is not free consulting.** Exchange offers *interpretation* — what PRISM observed and what it might mean. Exchange does not offer remediation. Remediation stays behind the engagement. Reps who give away solutions in the first call destroy commercial leverage without creating reciprocal obligation.

**Exchange is not interrogation disguised as insight.** Opening with "we've seen this in three accounts like yours, does this happen to you" is interrogation with a data preamble. Exchange requires the insight to be *specific to the buyer's business*, not pattern-matched from other accounts. PRISM's purpose is to enable that specificity.

## 2.3 When Exchange works

Exchange is at its strongest when:

- PRISM evidence is high-confidence and specific to the buyer's business
- The buyer is senior enough to value perspective over process
- The category is commercially complex (multi-stakeholder, multi-KPI)
- The buyer has likely done AI-assisted pre-call research
- The problem is diagnostic (the right answer is not obvious) rather than purely transactional
- Cross-functional sensemaking is required

It is especially powerful in executive and cross-functional commerce conversations because buyers in those contexts need sensemaking, not feature narration.

## 2.4 When Exchange fails

Exchange is intolerant of specific conditions. Naming them protects reps from misapplying the methodology:

- **PRISM confidence is low.** Exchange collapses when the hypothesis is ungrounded. Default to SPIN-style discovery.
- **The buyer is hostile or openly skeptical** of outside inference. Exchange requires a baseline willingness to engage. If that baseline is absent, the buyer will weaponize every hypothesis as evidence the rep doesn't understand them. Default to motivational interviewing patterns — mirror, label, wait.
- **The meeting is explicitly technical scoping.** An SE running a technical deep-dive does not open with Exchange; they run SPIN-sequenced architectural discovery.
- **Procurement or paper-process qualification is the explicit agenda.** Exchange is inappropriate in a "we need pricing and contract terms by Friday" call.
- **The seller lacks the fluency to separate hypothesis from assertion.** Early-career reps under pressure will convert "I think X" into "X is happening," and the tentativeness token disappears. Exchange fails silently. This is a training problem, not a methodology problem — and it is the single largest failure mode at rollout.

## 2.5 Relationship to MEDDPICC

MEDDPICC tells Algolia what to qualify. Exchange tells the rep how to earn that information.

MEDDPICC is the deal-health checklist. It exposes skipped qualification — the reason it wins at deal reviews is that missing information becomes visible. It has never had a conversational engine fit for the 2024–2026 buyer. Its field-filling assumed a buyer who hadn't yet evaluated the category. That buyer is extinct.

Today's prospect arrives having researched Algolia, Constructor, Coveo, and Bloomreach in ChatGPT and Perplexity, having already scored 85% of their decision criteria, and having already placed a preferred vendor on the Day One shortlist 94% of the time. In that context, a rep running a MEDDPICC question bank sounds like someone cramming for an exam the buyer already passed.

**Discovery OS is the conversational engine.** PRISM assembles the site audit, exec commentary, tech stack, and financial signals a rep would previously have spent discovery calls extracting. Exchange turns those findings into calibrated hypotheses the buyer corrects — and every correction fills a MEDDPICC field with language the buyer *owns*.

- **M** (Metrics) gets filled when the rep stakes a hypothesis on a specific KPI — *"we think your search-driven conversion is running roughly X"* — and the buyer either confirms or supplies the correct number.
- **E** (Economic Buyer) gets identified when the Audit-to-Question Bridge asks *"whose number moves if we fix this?"* — sensemaking, not interrogation.
- **D** (Decision Criteria) emerges when the Competitive Mirror surfaces the real selection axis in the buyer's own words.
- **D** (Decision Process) surfaces through timing-and-sequence probes following trigger-event findings.
- **P** (Paper Process) is the one field where traditional qualification still works — Exchange doesn't meaningfully change procurement conversations.
- **I** (Identified Pain) is the field Exchange most transforms. The buyer *states* their pain in their own KPIs because the rep already staked a hypothesis on them.
- **C** (Champion) emerges from reciprocity, not qualification. Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment specifically is the champion-creating move.
- **C** (Competition) is pre-mapped by PRISM and confirmed by the Competitive Mirror in the opening minutes.

MEDDPICC stays. Discovery OS makes its fields want to fill themselves.

# 3. The Core Loop

**10-second version:** Prep → Open → Hypothesize → Branch → Deepen → Commit → Handoff. Each stage has explicit moves, explicit timing, and explicit branch logic. The loop is a structural backbone, not a script.

## 3.1 The full call arc

A first discovery call runs approximately 45 minutes. The loop allocates time as follows:

| Stage | Time | Primary activity | Primary moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prep** | Pre-call | PRISM review, hypothesis selection, opening move choice | — |
| **Open** | 0–5 min | Pre-empt competitive narrative, establish peer status, set shared agenda | F1–F6 (opening moves) |
| **Hypothesize** | 5–15 min | Deliver 2–3 Calibrated Hypotheses from PRISM findings | M2 (Calibrated Hypothesis), M1 (Permission Reveal) |
| **Branch** | 15–25 min | Classify buyer reaction; route to appropriate deepening path | M6 (Audit-to-Question Bridge), M8 (Status-Returning Question) |
| **Deepen** | 25–35 min | Follow the reaction-led branch into diagnosis; activate buying committee | M5 (Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment), M7 (Falsifiable Benchmark), M4 (Competitive Mirror) |
| **Commit** | 35–43 min | Specific next-step commitment with named attendees; champion narrative handoff | M10 (Deferred Prescription) |
| **Handoff** | Post-call | PRISM update, MEDDPICC fields, role-continuum transition | — |

## 3.2 Sequencing rules

**Rule 1 — Opening protocol (0–5 minutes).** Deploy exactly one of F1–F6 based on: primary competitor detected (from PRISM's tech-stack and competitive signals), archetype, and buyer persona.

- If Constructor is detected as primary competitor → F3 (Merchandiser Permission Reveal) is default, unless buyer is engineering-led
- If Coveo is detected → F2 (Category Redefinition)
- If homegrown/Elastic → F1 with an unsolved diagnostic pattern
- If Bloomreach → F6 (Competitive Mirror) with consolidation-risk surfacing
- If unclear → F5 (Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring)

**Rule 2 — Hypothesis delivery (5–15 minutes).** Deploy no more than three Calibrated Hypotheses per call, each from a different finding category (e.g., one search-audit, one exec-commentary, one tech-stack). Rank by Confidence × Business Impact. Never lead with a Low-Confidence or High-Risk-if-Wrong hypothesis.

**Rule 3 — Reaction classification (15–25 minutes).** Every buyer response to a hypothesis is classified into one of four reactions:

- **Confirms** → deploy QS-Deepen (how bad is it, who knows, what's been tried, what's the business cost)
- **Amends** → deploy QS-Reframe (take the amendment as new data; re-state the hypothesis in buyer-owned language)
- **Rejects** → deploy QS-Disarm (Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment + Status-Returning Question; do not argue)
- **Deflects** → deploy QS-Anchor (Falsifiable Benchmark to re-anchor with a second data point)

**Rule 4 — Escalation guardrail.** If two consecutive hypotheses are rejected, abandon Exchange for the remainder of the call and revert to SPIN-style discovery. This is not a failure — it is a signal that either the rep is out of domain depth or PRISM data is miscalibrated. Flag the call for post-call PRISM data QA.

**Rule 5 — Buying-committee activation (25–35 minutes).** Deploy buying-committee findings and the next-meeting hook. The next-meeting ask must include at least one stakeholder the buyer did not volunteer — sourced from PRISM's LinkedIn/hiring-signal data.

**Rule 6 — Closing protocol (last 5 minutes).** Deferred Prescription (resist giving away solutions), specific-next-step commit (date, attendees, agenda), Champion Narrative handoff (what the buyer will say internally to the skeptical stakeholder).

## 3.3 The single-page call plan

Every Exchange call runs against a single-page call plan PRISM produces. The plan contains, and only contains:

1. **Opening move** (F1–F6), pre-selected
2. **Three ranked hypotheses**, each with metadata (Confidence, Risk-if-wrong, Evidence grounding)
3. **Branch tree** — anticipated Confirms / Amends / Rejects / Deflects reactions, each with specific deepening question
4. **One likely competitive objection** with named response pattern
5. **One cost-of-inaction cue** tied to the highest-confidence hypothesis
6. **One recommended stakeholder** to add for the next meeting
7. **One next-meeting hook** — specific, calendar-able, with a named deliverable

If PRISM outputs more than that for the first 45 minutes of a call, it is not compressed enough for field adoption. The single page is the compression discipline.

# 4. The Moves Library

**10-second version:** 16 named structural maneuvers — 10 core moves (M1–M10) plus 6 opening moves (F1–F6). Each has a 10-second card, a named failure mode, and an Algolia first-call example. These are structural, not scripted. The names matter; the exact words do not.

---

## 4.1 Core moves (M1–M10)

### M1 — Permission Reveal 🟢

**Mechanism:** Before disclosing pre-call findings, ask explicit consent and frame the deliverable as hypothesis, not verdict.

**Source:** SPIKES-I (Baile et al. 2000) + MI autonomy (Miller & Rollnick 2013).

**10-second card:** *Ask before you tell. Name the work, frame it as hypothesis, give them the veto.*

**Failure mode:** Sounds performative if followed by a pitch instead of curiosity. If the rep "asks permission" and then delivers a deck, Permission Reveal has been weaponized into stage setting.

**Algolia example (fashion merch VP):** "Before we get into anything, I ran ~40 queries on your site search last week and pulled three quotes from your last two earnings calls about conversion. Would it be useful if I walked you through the pattern I think I'm seeing — on the understanding that you have the inside view and I'm probably half-right?"

---

### M2 — Calibrated Hypothesis 🟡 core / 🟢 analog

**Mechanism:** A single declarative statement structured as: [observed signal] + [mechanical inference] + [tentative business consequence] + [invitation to correct]. The tentativeness token is structural — Rackham (1988) 🟢 found that flat assertions in major sales correlate with objections, not advances.

**Source:** Consulting hypothesis-first method + MI analog.

**10-second card:** *One hypothesis, named evidence, tentative verb, explicit invitation. "I think X because Y — does that track?"*

**Failure mode:** Over-certainty. If the rep frames the hypothesis as fact, the buyer defends rather than discloses. Equally, excessive hedging ("kind of, maybe, I think we might") correlates with lower outcome rates (Chorus.ai 🟡). The sweet spot is calibrated confidence: specific evidence + tentative verb.

**Algolia example (grocery product-search owner):** "You added same-day delivery zip codes in Q3 per the press release, and your site search still routes 'milk' to the generic PLP before delivery-eligible results. My guess: out-of-stock suppression runs ahead of your delivery-window filter, which would cost roughly the lift same-day was supposed to deliver. I could be wrong about the sequence — how close is that?"

---

### M3 — Peer-Status Anchor 🟡

**Mechanism:** In the first two minutes, frame the meeting as peer-to-peer fit assessment, not vendor audition. Give the buyer an early real "out."

**Source:** Sandler upfront contracts + Enns (2010) peer-fit framing + Khalsa & Illig (2008).

**10-second card:** *Open with "let's see if we fit," not "let me show you what we do." Give them the out.*

**Failure mode:** Comes off as chest-beating if the rep oversells their expertise or treats "peer" status as an assertion rather than something earned through the hypothesis that follows.

**Algolia example:** "My job for the next 30 minutes isn't to pitch Algolia — it's to figure out with you whether what we do maps to what's breaking for your team. If 15 minutes in we both think it's not a fit, I'd rather we say so than run the full meeting."

---

### M4 — Competitive Mirror 🟡

**Mechanism:** Name what a specific peer company does differently — without ranking or editorializing on "ahead." Ask the buyer to attribute motive.

**Source:** Challenger constructive tension (Dixon & Adamson 2011) + two-sided messaging research (Cialdini).

**10-second card:** *Name what a specific peer is doing. Don't rank. Ask them to explain the gap.*

**Failure mode:** Triggers defensiveness if used as rebuttal bait. If the rep's framing implies "look how behind you are," Competitive Mirror fails. The frame must be neutral observation, not judgment.

**Algolia example:** "Sephora moved their product-attribute query parsing to vector+keyword hybrid about 18 months ago — you can see it in how 'red lipstick matte drugstore-dupe' resolves on their site versus yours. I don't know if that's right for your margin structure. How do you think about that tradeoff for your category?"

---

### M5 — Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment 🟢

**Mechanism:** Name the likely prior scar without claiming to know the buyer's history; underclaim specifics; pause.

**Source:** Voss (2016) accusation audit + MI empathy (Miller & Rollnick) + ORBIT rapport research (Alison et al. 2013).

**10-second card:** *Name the scar they didn't mention. Underclaim. Pause.*

**Failure mode:** Feels manipulative if the wound is pure inference without evidence. Must be grounded in at least one observable signal from PRISM (Glassdoor reviews, LinkedIn role-churn patterns, GitHub archaeology, or industry pattern).

**Algolia example:** "My hunch is the last time someone tried to fix relevance at your company, it turned into a nine-month roadmap item that got deprioritized when Black Friday came — and now 'search' has a reputation internally as something that never quite works. That might be totally off; something like that is pretty common at this stage of digital maturity."

---

### M6 — Audit-to-Question Bridge 🟢

**Mechanism:** Pair every external finding with an internal-context question only the buyer can answer. The external observation earns the right to the internal question.

**Source:** SPIKES S→P→K→E sequence + SPIN implication logic + clinical history-taking.

**10-second card:** *Every external finding buys one internal question. Spend the coin.*

**Failure mode:** Becomes interrogation if repeated mechanically ("and another thing we noticed... and another thing..."). The bridge must pivot — after two findings, move to a deepening question.

**Algolia example:** "Site-wide, about 17% of sessions end on a zero-results page for queries with three or more tokens — that's the external view. What I can't see: does that show up in your team's week? Is it surfaced by merch, by e-comm, or is it invisible until QBR?"

---

### M7 — Falsifiable Benchmark 🟡 / 🔵

**Mechanism:** Offer a quantitative anchor with the uncertainty band *explicitly named*. The error bar is the reciprocity gift — it signals the rep is not trying to win a number game.

**Source:** Value-selling literature + calibrated-confidence research from forecasting (Tetlock).

**10-second card:** *Anchor a number with an explicit error bar. The uncertainty IS the gift.*

**Failure mode:** Turns into fake math if too many assumptions are hidden or the range is so wide as to be meaningless ("somewhere between $100K and $10M"). The band must be defensible and the assumptions must be stated.

**Algolia example:** "In fashion-retail benchmark cohorts I've seen (n≈20, mid-market mix, take with salt), search-driven revenue runs 22–38% of total digital. Your public numbers suggest low end. I'm inferring from traffic-mix proxies, not your real attribution — right zip code?"

---

### M8 — Status-Returning Question 🟢

**Mechanism:** After a hypothesis is rejected or deflected, hand control back to the buyer with an open question that respects their expertise. Do not argue. Do not re-assert.

**Source:** MI rolling-with-resistance + Voss calibrated questions + ORBIT autonomy-granting.

**10-second card:** *When they push back, hand back the mic. No defense.*

**Failure mode:** Weak reps use Status-Returning Question as a hedge for lack of conviction — framing every statement as a question. The move is reserved for rejection recovery, not universal framing.

**Algolia example (after buyer rejects hypothesis):** "Fair — I was working from outside signals, and you have the inside view. What would you have told me, if I'd asked before making that call?"

---

### M9 — Documented-Fallibility Disclosure 🔵

**Mechanism:** When PRISM confidence is low or risk-if-wrong is high, explicitly label the hypothesis as low-confidence before delivering it. Signals calibrated judgment rather than false certainty.

**Source:** Calibrated-confidence research (Tetlock *Superforecasting* 2015) + academic hedging norms.

**10-second card:** *When you might be wrong, say so before saying it.*

**Failure mode:** Over-use. If the rep hedges every hypothesis, all signal is lost. Reserve for High-Risk-if-Wrong hypotheses — the ones where being wrong burns credibility for the rest of the call.

**Algolia example:** "I'm going to say something I might be completely wrong about, and I'd rather ask than assume — but it looks from outside like your search team has had one or two re-platform attempts stall in the last two years. If that's not true, please tell me and I'll drop it."

---

### M10 — Deferred Prescription 🟡

**Mechanism:** When the buyer asks pricing, solutioning, or specifics in the first half of the call, defer with a named reason rather than providing a guess. Protects credibility and deal economics.

**Source:** Gong research on pricing timing 🟡 (discovery calls surfacing pricing after minute 38–46 correlate with closed-won; calls introducing pricing in first 15 minutes correlate with stalled deals).

**10-second card:** *Don't quote early. Name the reason you won't.*

**Failure mode:** Sounds like the rep doesn't know. The deferral must be framed as protecting the buyer ("the range I'd give without knowing X would be wrong in a way that costs you credibility upstairs") not as vendor evasion.

**Algolia example:** "I could give you a pricing range now, but the range I'd give without knowing your query volume, catalog size, and traffic profile would be wrong in a way that costs you credibility when you bring it to Finance. Let me get one of those numbers and I'll give you a tight range today."

## 4.2 Opening moves (F1–F6) — the first five minutes

These are the highest-leverage moves in the methodology. The first five minutes of a first call determines whether the rep has permission to run Exchange for the next 40 minutes. All six moves are designed to pre-empt competitive framing — especially the "Algolia is a developer tool" narrative — before the prospect raises it.

### F1 — Diagnostic Preemption 🔵

**Mechanism:** Open without self-introduction; deliver three PRISM findings in 90 seconds; invite pushback before anything else.

**Source:** McKinsey hypothesis-first meeting protocol + Cialdini reciprocity + Burgoon expectancy-violation theory.

**10-second card:** *Arrive with three hypotheses. Ask for pushback before anything else.*

**When to deploy:** Best when PRISM confidence is high and buyer is senior (VP+). Risky with early-career buyers who expect process rigor. Do not deploy if audit data is shaky.

**Algolia example:** "Sarah, before we begin — I'll skip the intro and share three hypotheses I formed from your site last night. (1) Your 'black dress' search returns 847 results, and 12 of the first 20 are out-of-stock — I suspect OOS burial requires a dev ticket. (2) Your zero-result rate on brand searches is ~4%, three times industry — usually a PIM synonym issue. (3) Your search-driven revenue share is likely 35–40% of digital, but you have no A/B framework to prove incremental lift. If any are wrong, I want to know. If right, let's spend the call on those three problems — not a demo."

---

### F2 — Category Redefinition 🔵

**Mechanism:** Never accept the default frame of "commerce search." Name a broader category — *discovery infrastructure for AI-era commerce* — and evaluate competitors inside that frame.

**Source:** April Dunford category-design practice + strategic positioning literature.

**10-second card:** *Name a bigger category than "commerce search" — before they put you in the smaller one.*

**When to deploy:** Critical against Coveo (who will anchor on enterprise-breadth) and against Bloomreach (who will anchor on suite-consolidation). Less necessary against pure Constructor competition.

**Algolia example:** "Quick framing note: 'commerce search' is about to stop mattering as a category. Shoppers arrive via ChatGPT, agentic checkout, and native app surfaces — not just site search. The category that matters is *discovery infrastructure*: a composable relevance layer serving your site, apps, agents, and AI surfaces from one index. Constructor, Bloomreach, Coveo are excellent at the old category. What I want to spend this call on is whether the new category matters to your 18-month roadmap."

---

### F3 — Merchandiser Permission Reveal 🔵

**Mechanism:** Name the narrative the prospect has likely been told ("Algolia is a developer tool"), ask permission to challenge it, then challenge with a live demonstration — not a slide.

**Source:** SPIKES + Brehm reactance theory (forbidden fruit effect — acknowledging the narrative deflates its power).

**10-second card:** *Name the narrative, ask permission, then challenge with evidence not rhetoric.*

**When to deploy:** Default move when Constructor is detected as primary competitor. Also when buyer is merch-led (rather than engineering-led).

**Algolia example:** "You've probably heard — maybe from Constructor, maybe from an analyst, maybe from a peer — that Algolia is a developer tool and Constructor is the merchandiser tool. That narrative is real and I'm not going to pretend it isn't. What I'd like to do in the next ten minutes is show you Merchandising Studio live on your catalog — and at the end, you tell me whether the narrative still holds. Is that a fair structure for the opening?"

---

### F4 — Peer-Status Anchor (opening-move variant) 🔵

**Mechanism:** Open with 2–3 named Algolia customers matching the prospect's industry, revenue, catalog size, and tech stack — before they ask for references.

**Source:** Cialdini social proof (peer-segment variant).

**10-second card:** *Open with a named peer before they ask for references.*

**When to deploy:** Universal — works for any archetype. Particularly strong when Algolia has a direct-competitor peer reference (e.g., in a DSW call, lead with Shoe Carnival).

**Algolia example:** "Before we get into diagnostics, two names that will probably shape how we talk: Shoe Carnival runs Algolia at your catalog scale and roughly your mix, and lifted search conversion 3.5×. Second, [peer brand] runs us at slightly larger scale and went live in 14 weeks. I mention them now because what I want to talk about is your situation specifically, and those are the closest comparable stories I have — so if you want to dig into either, we can."

---

### F5 — Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring 🔵

**Mechanism:** Share a PRISM-grounded draft agenda on screen; invite the buyer to edit; treat their edits as binding.

**Source:** Schein humble inquiry + Ben Franklin effect (asking for a small favor builds commitment).

**10-second card:** *Give them a draft agenda and ask them to edit it.*

**When to deploy:** Best when buyer seniority is ambiguous, when buying-committee dynamics are unclear, or when the buyer is new-in-role (e.g., new CEO / new CDO). Low-risk move — works across all archetypes.

**Algolia example:** "I drafted a three-part agenda for today based on what I saw in PRISM — want to put it on screen for two minutes and have you edit it? I'd rather build the call around what you actually need than what I assumed. Here's what I had..."

### F6 — Competitive Mirror (opening-move variant) 🔵

**Mechanism:** Name 2–3 competitors in the opening with specific strengths acknowledged honestly, then name Algolia's one material advantage for this specific prospect.

**Source:** Cialdini pre-suasion + two-sided messaging research.

**10-second card:** *Name each competitor's real strength before you name yours.*

**When to deploy:** Strong universal move, but especially when PRISM detects multiple competitors in evaluation. Particularly effective when Algolia is rumored to be a "credible #2" — this move can move the rep from #2 to #1 in the first five minutes.

**Algolia example:** "You're likely evaluating us against Constructor, Coveo, and maybe Bloomreach. Honest read: if what you want is the most merchandiser-friendly UI out of the box, Constructor is genuinely strong. If you want the most enterprise-breadth across commerce + service + workplace, Coveo has depth. Where we tend to win is when the buyer cares about composable architecture and speed-to-iterate — because our APIs are designed for that. Is that the axis you're optimizing on, or a different one?"

## 4.3 Universal output archetypes

Every move in the library belongs to one of six archetypes. The archetypes are a cognitive shortcut — the rep, scanning the PRISM call plan, asks: *"What kind of move does this call for?"*

- **Teach** — use when the finding is high-confidence and non-threatening (e.g., category framing, peer benchmark)
- **Test** — use when the finding is plausible but uncertain (the default mode for most Calibrated Hypotheses)
- **Triage** — use when multiple findings exist but the buyer's attention is limited (pick one; hold the rest)
- **Acknowledge** — use when resistance or wound patterns are present (Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment before any teaching)
- **Translate** — use when a technical finding must be explained in business language (typical for exec calls)
- **Escalate** — use when the finding has executive relevance and should shape stakeholder strategy (triggers BC activation)

PRISM should tag every output with one archetype. The rep learns the archetypes once; the individual moves follow from the archetype.

## 4.4 Branch selection rules

When a Calibrated Hypothesis lands, the rep classifies the buyer's reaction and selects the next move:

- **If buyer confirms the hypothesis** → burrow deeper into consequence and ownership (M6 Audit-to-Question Bridge)
- **If buyer partially confirms / amends** → narrow scope and ask for the one missing variable
- **If buyer denies / rejects** → M8 Status-Returning Question + consider M5 Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment
- **If buyer shows scar tissue** → switch to M5 Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment before any further teaching
- **If buyer pulls technical** → move from outcome narrative to architecture consequences without losing business frame
- **If buyer raises competition** → trigger M4 Competitive Mirror and identify the real selection axis
- **If buyer wants price early** → M10 Deferred Prescription
- **If buyer is an executive** → suppress ROI claims; lead with cost-of-inaction

# 5. The Competitive Pre-Empt Playbook

**10-second version:** The "Algolia is a developer tool / Constructor is for business users" narrative is pre-planted in most enterprise calls. It cannot be rebutted defensively — rebuttal strengthens the narrative. It must be pre-empted in the first five minutes via specific moves grounded in buyer-specific PRISM data. This section documents the 15-objection pre-map and the competitor-specific opening strategies.

## 5.1 The narrative landscape

Every competitor plants a narrative. The methodology addresses each explicitly:

- **Constructor.** "Built for merchandisers, not engineers." "Revenue-guaranteed Proof Schedule." Aggressive merchandiser-usability marketing. Recently strong at enterprise deals Algolia has lost.
- **Coveo.** "Spinal AI platform across commerce + service + workplace." "17 years of AI." Strong Salesforce pull. Enterprise-breadth positioning.
- **Bloomreach.** "Limitless commerce personalization." Engagement/CDP bundle. "25% of US/UK retail ecommerce" scale claim. Suite-consolidation pitch.

## 5.3 Competitor-specific first-five-minute strategies

For each primary competitor detected by PRISM, Discovery OS prescribes a default opening-move combination.

**vs. Constructor (primary case).** F1 (Diagnostic Preemption with PRISM merchandiser-gap findings) + F3 (Merchandiser Permission Reveal naming the narrative) + live Merchandising Studio within 10 minutes. Critical: never defend "relevance" as a word. Substitute "outcome-ranked discovery." The word "relevance" concedes the narrative that Algolia is behind-the-scenes technology. The word "discovery" positions Algolia as merchandiser-facing.

**vs. Coveo.** F2 (Category Redefinition to composable discovery infrastructure) + "whose number moves if commerce search works?" question. Honest acknowledgment of Coveo's multi-use-case strength is load-bearing — trying to out-enterprise Coveo is a losing frame. Reframe instead to composability and speed-to-iterate.

**vs. Bloomreach.** F6 (Competitive Mirror) + Consolidation-Risk Surfacing. Probe whether a CDP is already in place — if Segment, mParticle, or Lytics is in the stack, the Bloomreach-Engagement bundle math collapses and the deal becomes a pure commerce-search comparison where Algolia is strong.

**vs. Elastic / homegrown.** F1 with a visible Elastic-unsolved diagnostic pattern on their site (e.g., typo tolerance, semantic query parsing) + Engineering Opportunity-Cost Mirror. Agree on build capability ("yes, you can build this"); pivot to roadmap opportunity cost ("what else are those engineers not shipping?").

**vs. Athos (Klevu + Searchspring).** F4 (Peer-Status Anchor) with a same-size brand that migrated off Klevu or Searchspring, + live Merchandising Studio demo. Acknowledge post-merger integration uncertainty honestly. Don't pretend it's not a concern; name it and offer to help the buyer think through the risk.

**Unknown / no primary competitor detected.** F5 (Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring) — lowest-risk default when competitive landscape is ambiguous.

## 5.4 The "developer tool" landmine — tactical treatment

This landmine deserves its own treatment because it is the single most consequential competitive narrative Algolia reps face. It is planted by Constructor (primarily), reinforced by analyst coverage, and often carried into the first call by the buyer without the buyer even knowing they absorbed it.

The instinctive rep move is to rebut: "Actually, Algolia has a Merchandising Studio that works without engineering." This move strengthens the narrative. By defending the accusation, the rep implicitly accepts its frame.

The methodology move is to acknowledge and redirect:

1. **Acknowledge the narrative exists.** "You've probably heard that Algolia is a developer tool. That narrative is real."
2. **Don't defend the word.** Reframe from "developer vs. business user" to "what the merch team actually needs to ship today."
3. **Demonstrate, don't promise.** Ship a merchandising rule live on the buyer's catalog within ten minutes. The demonstration kills the narrative faster than any argument.
4. **Let the buyer say the conclusion.** Exchange requires the buyer to arrive at the reframe themselves. The rep sets up the conditions; the buyer draws the conclusion. When the buyer says "actually that was faster than I expected," the narrative is dead.

# 6. The Skeptical-AE Pre-Map

**10-second version:** Top-performing AEs will push back on Discovery OS harder than any prospect. If rollout can't survive their objections, the methodology dies in week one. This section maps the predictable objections and the responses.

## 6.1 Why this section exists

Most sales methodologies fail at rollout, not at design. The failure pattern is consistent: a senior leader introduces a new framework, top-performing AEs conclude it's either (a) theory that doesn't fit real deals, (b) a threat to their existing process, or (c) enablement theater. Adoption craters. Six months later, the framework quietly disappears from the sales floor.

The top 10% of AEs matter most here. They are the ones whose deals actually close, whose adoption signals the rest of the team, and whose rejection kills momentum fastest. This section documents the specific objections those AEs will raise against Discovery OS — and the responses.

Every response is grounded in either evidence or acknowledgment. Where the objection has merit, the response concedes and qualifies. Where the objection is wrong, the response refutes without dismissing.

## 6.2 The 10 predictable objections

### Objection 1: "I already do discovery well. Why do I need this?"

**The real claim:** "My win rate is strong. This is a solution in search of a problem."

**Response:** Probably true. Discovery OS is not a replacement for strong rep judgment — it is a multiplier for it. The specific question to ask a top performer is: *"In the last 10 deals, how many times did you walk in cold vs. walk in with a PRISM-level pre-call intelligence packet?"* If the answer is "I did my own research" — Discovery OS gives them a structured way to leverage that research. If the answer is "we walked in cold" — Discovery OS changes what first calls can be. Either way, the methodology augments their current practice; it doesn't replace it.

### Objection 2: "My buyers hate hypothesis-heavy openings. They want to feel heard."

**The real claim:** "Exchange sounds like I'm talking over the buyer."

**Response:** Valid concern, and the methodology explicitly addresses it. Exchange is *not* rep-talks-first; it is rep-asks-permission-first. The Permission Reveal (M1) is structurally required before any Calibrated Hypothesis. If the rep is talking without invitation, they're running pitch-mode, not Exchange. The 10-second card for M1 is literally: *"Ask before you tell."* If the buyer doesn't want a hypothesis, the rep doesn't deliver one — they revert to SPIN. This is encoded in the escalation guardrail.

### Objection 3: "PRISM data is going to be wrong sometimes. I'll look stupid."

**The real claim:** "I'm not going to stake my credibility on data I didn't produce."

**Response:** The single most important objection. Two answers:

First, Discovery OS explicitly requires Calibrated Hypotheses — not assertions. The tentativeness token ("I could be wrong about the sequence, but the public data suggests...") is structural, not decorative. When PRISM is wrong, the rep says "thanks for the correction" and the hypothesis becomes a question. That's the design, not a failure mode.

Second, PRISM data QA is a precondition for rollout. Algolia will not deploy a methodology that relies on unvalidated intelligence. Before Discovery OS ships, PRISM will have (a) confidence scoring on every finding, (b) evidence-grounding URLs for every claim, (c) reviewer workflow for high-risk-if-wrong outputs. Reps will never open a call with Low-Confidence × High-Risk-if-Wrong findings — those are gated. This is Deliverable 9's explicit requirement.

### Objection 4: "This is going to slow me down. I have pipeline targets."

**The real claim:** "Pre-call prep is expensive; I already don't have time."

**Response:** Discovery OS is designed for single-page consumption. The rep's entire call plan is one page: opening move, three ranked hypotheses, branch tree, one objection response, one next-meeting hook. Compressed. If PRISM is outputting more than that, PRISM is broken, not the methodology. Pre-call prep time should be 5–10 minutes per call, not an hour. Post-call MEDDPICC field-filling should be faster than today because the fields naturally fill during Exchange rather than requiring separate extraction.

### Objection 5: "I'm worried this will only work for senior reps who can improvise."

**The real claim:** "This sounds like expert-level craft. My BDRs and new AEs will butcher it."

**Response:** Correct — and the methodology is deliberately scaffolded for this. Discovery OS has two delivery modes:

- **Scaffolded mode** (for junior reps): more scripted; uses a smaller subset of moves (M1 + M2 + M6 + M10); falls back to SPIN after two rejected hypotheses; relies more heavily on PRISM's pre-generated outputs.
- **Full mode** (for senior reps): full moves library; more improvisational branching; higher expected hypothesis-rejection recovery.

Enablement will certify reps to Full Mode based on demonstrated proficiency. New reps run Scaffolded Mode for their first 90 days. This is in the Training Outline (§10).

### Objection 6: "What if my prospect is a BDR themselves and arrives with a question bank?"

**The real claim:** "What if the methodology fails against traditional procurement?"

**Response:** Exchange is inappropriate for procurement-led, regulated, or RFP-driven calls. The methodology explicitly names this as a failure condition (§2.4). When procurement takes over, reps revert to process-driven qualification. Discovery OS is a first-call and early-stage methodology; it is not a universal replacement for the full sales motion.

### Objection 7: "This is just Challenger rebranded."

**The real claim:** "Nothing new here."

**Response:** Direct refutation. Challenger seeks a "never-thought-of-it-that-way" moment driven by seller-superior insight. Exchange seeks a *correction* — the buyer teaching back. Challenger optimizes for rep control and constructive tension; Exchange optimizes for buyer elaboration and reciprocal disclosure. The directionality is opposite. A rep who runs Challenger-style calls under the Discovery OS banner is doing it wrong, and this is the single most common misapplication we expect to see. Enablement will specifically train against this confusion.

### Objection 8: "I don't see Gong data on this specific methodology."

**The real claim:** "Prove it works."

**Response:** Correct — no published Gong study has isolated Exchange vs. question-led discovery in A/B form because nobody has had PRISM before. What we have is (a) strong Gong data on the adjacent behaviors Exchange requires (talk ratio, question density, insight delivery, pricing timing, multithreading); (b) strong Gartner data on AI-era buyer expectations; (c) strong academic evidence on reciprocity and disclosure. The methodology is a reasoned synthesis, tagged 🔵 where we infer and 🟢 where we don't. Algolia will instrument its own A/B testing within the first 90 days of rollout to move the methodology from 🔵 to 🟢. If you want to be the rep whose calls establish the baseline, this is your opening.

### Objection 9: "My manager is going to coach me on MEDDPICC fields. How does this fit?"

**The real claim:** "Whose system is this?"

**Response:** MEDDPICC stays. Your manager will still review MEDDPICC completeness at deal reviews. What Discovery OS changes is *how you fill the fields*. Instead of running a MEDDPICC question bank and extracting answers, you run Exchange and the fields fill themselves through reciprocal dialog. Your manager will see better-filled MEDDPICC records — specifically, Metrics stated in the buyer's own numbers, Identified Pain in the buyer's own KPI language, and Champion relationships with more texture. MEDDPICC is the scorecard; Exchange is how you play the game.

### Objection 10: "What happens when PRISM doesn't have enough data on a small or private prospect?"

**The real claim:** "Exchange requires rich pre-call intelligence. Not all deals have that."

**Response:** Correct. Exchange is intolerant of low PRISM confidence. When PRISM can't produce high-confidence findings, the rep defaults to SPIN-style discovery. This is named in the escalation guardrail (§3.2, Rule 4). For small private prospects where PRISM data is thin, the methodology explicitly recommends question-led opening. Discovery OS is not one-size-fits-all; it is data-dependent.

## 6.3 The hidden objection

The objection no AE will state out loud, but that underlies several of the above: *"This methodology makes me more dependent on PRISM — and more accountable when PRISM is wrong."*

This is true. Discovery OS shifts rep accountability from "did you ask the right questions" to "did you deploy the right hypothesis at the right confidence level." It creates a new kind of coaching burden: managers now coach both rep behavior *and* PRISM usage.

The honest answer: yes, this creates new accountability. It also gives top performers a 3–5× intelligence advantage over reps at competitors still running cold. That tradeoff is what makes the methodology worth adopting. Top AEs will generally accept new accountability in exchange for structural advantage. The rollout conversation should name this tradeoff explicitly rather than pretending it isn't there.

# 7. The Role Continuum (Preliminary)

**10-second version:** Discovery is not one-call. It compounds across BDR/XDR → AE → SE → CS over the full deal lifecycle. Each role owns a different layer of discovery; PRISM data compounds rather than resets between roles. This section sketches the arc at a preliminary level — full build-out is a separate workstream.

## 7.1 Why this section is preliminary

The research passes that produced Discovery OS v1 focused on the AE first-call because that is where the exchange paradigm's novelty is most concentrated. A full treatment of the role continuum — including SE technical discovery, CS transition discovery, and the specific handoff protocols between roles — requires dedicated research (Pass D in the original architecture). This section documents the preliminary structure so the v1 methodology is not AE-only, but it is explicitly incomplete.

## 7.2 The four roles

### BDR / XDR

**Discovery owned:** Pre-call qualification. Entity fit. Initial intent signal. Economic-buyer proxy identification.

**Exchange adaptation:** BDRs do not run full Exchange — the venue (email, LinkedIn, cold call) does not support it. BDRs use *micro-Exchange*: a single PRISM-grounded insight in the outbound message, followed by a single calibrated question. The goal is not diagnosis; it is earning the meeting.

**Example outbound:** *"I spent 15 minutes on [brand]'s site yesterday. Your zero-result rate on branded queries runs ~4% — three times industry average. Usually a PIM synonym issue, sometimes a deeper catalog problem. If that's the kind of thing that matters to your team right now, worth a 15-minute call to talk about it?"*

**Handoff to AE:** PRISM records the BDR's hypothesis, the buyer's response (if any), and flags which PRISM findings were used. The AE arrives with context, not a blank slate.

### AE

**Discovery owned:** Problem diagnosis. Business-impact framing. Champion identification. Economic-buyer qualification. Competitive landscape.

**Exchange adaptation:** Full methodology. First call runs the complete Prep → Open → Hypothesize → Branch → Deepen → Commit arc. Follow-up calls extend the branch logic into MEDDPICC field-filling.

**Handoff to SE:** PRISM records which hypotheses confirmed, which amended, which rejected. The SE arrives knowing what the buyer believes about their own business — not guessing at it.

### SE

**Discovery owned:** Technical architecture. Implementation complexity. Integration path. Data-quality diagnosis. Feasibility validation. Engineering-buyer trust.

**Exchange adaptation:** SE discovery is usually the second or third call. Exchange applies differently: the SE enters with PRISM's tech-stack inference *and* the AE's earlier hypothesis confirmations. The SE's hypothesis is architectural ("your Dynamic Yield is installed but not wired to search; we think the integration failure is in the events layer, not the ranking layer"). Buyer corrects or confirms. Same structure, different domain.

**Handoff to AE (back):** SE records architectural findings, feasibility assessment, and any technical-buyer objections that shifted the deal's commercial framing.

### CS / Post-Sale

**Discovery owned:** Implementation success criteria. Expansion use-case identification. Champion succession. Renewal risk factors.

**Exchange adaptation:** Post-sale Exchange is about value realization. The CS rep opens with a PRISM-generated "we expected your search conversion to move from X to Y in the first 90 days — here's what we're seeing" hypothesis. Buyer confirms, amends, or rejects. Same paradigm, applied to post-sale value delivery.

## 7.3 The compounding principle

The most important structural feature of the role continuum is that **PRISM intelligence compounds across roles rather than resetting.**

In the legacy model, each role restarts discovery. The BDR asks generic qualifying questions; the AE asks the same questions again on the first call; the SE asks the same questions again from a technical angle; the CS rep asks the same questions again at onboarding. The buyer experiences this as interrogation, and each repetition burns credibility.

In the Discovery OS model, PRISM maintains a *deal-intelligence object* that every role contributes to and reads from. The BDR's outbound hypothesis and the buyer's response is recorded. The AE arrives knowing what the buyer already partially confirmed. The SE arrives with the AE's pain diagnosis pre-loaded. The CS rep arrives knowing the exact success criteria the buyer committed to during the deal.

No question is asked twice. Every role knows what the previous role learned. PRISM is the memory.

This is the deepest structural change Discovery OS introduces, and it is also the one that requires the most PRISM engineering work. The v1 methodology documents the principle; Pass D will document the handoff protocols in detail; the PRISM v2 build will implement the deal-intelligence object.

## 7.4 Open questions for full role-continuum research (Pass D)

- What is the exact data contract between roles (what must each role record for the next role to use)?
- How does PRISM handle contradictory hypotheses confirmed by different roles?
- What is the coaching architecture for SE-specific Exchange vs. AE-specific Exchange?
- How does CS-triggered expansion discovery differ from net-new discovery?
- What is the handoff protocol when the deal cycles back to AE from SE (common pattern)?

These are deferred to the Pass D research workstream.

# 8. The Measurement Stub

**10-second version:** You can't codify what you can't measure. This stub defines the rubric, leading indicators, and coaching architecture for Discovery OS — preliminary enough to start instrumenting immediately, not comprehensive enough to ship as final. Full measurement is Pass E.

## 8.1 What makes a discovery call "good"?

The measurement framework separates *behavioral quality* (did the rep run Exchange correctly?) from *outcome quality* (did the call advance the deal?). Both matter; they are not the same.

**Behavioral quality indicators** are things that are observable in real-time or via call recording:

- Rep deployed one of F1–F6 in the first five minutes
- Rep delivered 2–3 Calibrated Hypotheses with tentativeness tokens
- Rep classified buyer reactions (confirms / amends / rejects / deflects) and routed appropriately
- Rep held silence >3 seconds after open-ended questions
- Rep referenced at least one PRISM finding by name in the first 90 seconds
- Rep talk-to-listen ratio fell within 35–50% (target 43%) 🟡 (Gong)
- No continuous rep monologue exceeded 90 seconds
- Rep deployed M10 (Deferred Prescription) if pricing was raised early
- Rep ended with specific calendar-booked next step
- Rep named at least one stakeholder the buyer did not volunteer

**Outcome quality indicators** are deal-state changes:

- Second meeting booked within 72 hours of first call
- Second meeting includes at least one new stakeholder
- MEDDPICC fields updated with buyer-owned language (not rep-extracted)
- Champion artifact produced (the narrative the buyer carries internally)
- Next-meeting agenda co-authored, not unilaterally set by rep

## 8.2 The 10-point call-scoring rubric (preliminary)

For post-call review. Managers score calls on these ten items. Scoring is binary (did it happen / didn't happen), not subjective.

1. Opening move (F1–F6) deployed in first five minutes — Y / N
2. At least one PRISM finding referenced by name — Y / N
3. 2–3 Calibrated Hypotheses delivered with tentativeness tokens — Y / N
4. Buyer reactions classified and routed (no robotic question sequence) — Y / N
5. Rep held silence ≥3 seconds after at least one open question — Y / N
6. No monologue exceeded 90 seconds — Y / N
7. Competitive narrative pre-empted or surfaced honestly — Y / N
8. Deferred Prescription deployed if pricing raised early — Y / N
9. Specific calendar-booked next step with named attendees — Y / N
10. At least one unvolunteered stakeholder added to next meeting — Y / N

A call scoring 8+ is an Exchange call. 5–7 is a hybrid. Below 5 is interrogation.

## 8.3 Leading indicators

Metrics that predict Discovery OS adoption is working, before win-rate data is available:

- **Second-meeting booking rate.** Target: 60%+ of first calls book a specific next step. Baseline in most orgs is 30–40%.
- **Multithreading count.** Target: by second meeting, ≥3 stakeholders engaged. Baseline 1–2.
- **MEDDPICC field-completion quality.** Target: Metrics and Identified Pain fields contain buyer-stated numbers and buyer-stated pain language (not rep-extracted approximations).
- **Pricing-timing shift.** Target: median first-pricing-mention moves from minute <15 to minute >35.
- **Call-plan compliance.** Target: 80%+ of first calls follow PRISM's single-page call plan.

## 8.4 Coaching architecture

Managers coach to Discovery OS via three mechanisms:

**Weekly call review.** One first-call per rep per week, scored against the 10-point rubric. Not a grade — a diagnostic. Identifies which moves the rep is executing and which are absent.

**Hypothesis quality review.** PRISM outputs a "hypothesis delivery log" per rep — what hypotheses they opened with, which confirmed, which amended, which rejected. Patterns reveal whether the rep is hedging too hard, asserting too hard, or picking the wrong finding to lead with.

**Role-continuum handoff review.** Post-sale handoff quality (did the SE arrive knowing what the AE learned?) is a deal-health leading indicator. Handoff quality predicts CS-stage expansion 🔵.

## 8.5 What's missing from v1 measurement

Named explicitly so nobody expects this section to be complete:

- Statistical validation of the 10-point rubric (which items most correlate with won deals?)
- Rep-level cohort analysis (does adoption correlate with win-rate lift at the rep level?)
- A/B test design for Exchange vs. SPIN-control openings
- Coaching cadence and intensity recommendations
- Compensation-structure implications (do we tie variable comp to call-plan compliance?)

Pass E addresses these.

# 9. The PRISM Translation Ruleset (Build Spec)

**10-second version:** This is the primary engineering endpoint of Discovery OS. It specifies how PRISM findings translate into methodology outputs — the schema, metadata, sequencing, and branch logic that PRISM v2 must implement. Build against this.

## 9.1 Philosophy

The methodology is the function. PRISM's data is the input. Executable rep outputs (questions, emails, narratives, objection responses, call plans) are the result of applying the function to the input.

PRISM v1 is a diagnosis engine — it produces findings. PRISM v2 must become a diagnosis-to-action engine — findings that translate, through Discovery OS, into the specific moves a rep should make.

Today, a rep reads the PRISM report and thinks "great, now what do I do with this?" After the refactor, the rep reads the PRISM output and the output itself tells them: *here's the hypothesis this implies, here's the exchange move that tests it, here's the objection it invites, here's the champion narrative it supports, here's the outbound email it produces.*

## 9.2 The schema

Every PRISM output is a **finding object** that translates into one or more **behavior objects**, with an associated **feedback object** that records buyer reaction and updates the deal-intelligence state.

### 9.2.1 Finding object

```
finding {
  category      → one of: SA, EC, TS, HS, FS, CA, BC, WB, TE, AC, BL, RG, CM
  evidence      → {type, payload, source_url, timestamp}
  confidence    → {High, Medium, Low}
  freshness     → days since signal captured
  archetype_fit → [Greenfield, Incumbent-displacement, Platform-consolidation, Composable-migration]
  persona_fit   → [Merch, Product, Eng, Exec]
  risk_if_wrong → {Low, Moderate, High}
}
```

### 9.2.2 Finding categories

| Code | Category | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SA** | Search audit gap | "Zero Personalization despite Dynamic Yield installed" |
| **EC** | Executive commentary | Earnings quote: "elevate our customers' omnichannel experience" |
| **TS** | Tech stack signal | BuiltWith shows Coveo Service Cloud; no commerce-search vendor detected |
| **HS** | Hiring signal | "Senior Merchandiser, Onsite Search" open 47 days |
| **FS** | Financial signal | Q3 digital-conversion miss; guidance cut; activist investor letter |
| **CA** | Competitive action | Incumbent vendor renewal window; PR on competitor adoption |
| **BC** | Buying-committee inference | SVP Digital vacant; new CDO started 3 months ago |
| **WB** | Wounded-buyer pattern | Two failed search re-platform attempts (LinkedIn + GitHub archaeology) |
| **TE** | Trigger event | New-CEO first 100 days; post-IPO; M&A close |
| **AC** | Archetype classification | Greenfield / Incumbent-displacement / etc. |
| **BL** | Brand-language gap (proposed) | Said-vs-Found mismatch between exec language and site behavior |
| **RG** | Regret signal (proposed) | G2/Reddit/LinkedIn evidence of incumbent dissatisfaction |
| **CM** | Category-momentum signal (proposed) | Peer-retailer adoption patterns in last 6 months |

### 9.2.3 Translation layer

```
translate(finding, persona, deal_context, competitive_context, confidence_threshold) →
  {
    recommended_opening_move  → F1–F6
    calibrated_hypothesis      → structured hypothesis statement
    exchange_move_sequence     → ordered list of M1–M10
    question_stack             → reaction-branched deepening questions
    objection_pre_map          → 1–3 most-likely objections with response patterns
    champion_narrative         → internal-carry narrative for the buyer
    next_meeting_hook          → specific, calendar-able next-step framing
    outbound_email_angle       → pre-meeting outreach angle
    disclosure_timing          → {opening, hypothesize, branch, deepen, commit}
    commercial_modeling_cue    → Falsifiable Benchmark parameters
  }
```

### 9.2.4 Behavior object

Every output carries mandatory metadata:

```
behavior_object {
  content            → the actual text/guidance
  type               → one of: H, EM, QS, OP, CN, OE, NH, DT
  confidence         → inherited from finding
  evidence_tier      → 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔵
  archetype_fit      → which archetypes this applies to
  persona_fit        → which personas this is tuned for
  risk_if_wrong      → Low / Moderate / High (High → requires M9 Documented-Fallibility)
  best_use_stage     → Open / Hypothesize / Branch / Deepen / Commit
  do_not_deploy_if   → list of disqualifying conditions
  visual_proof       → yes/no (does a screenshot or live query exist?)
  external_citation  → yes/no (is there a public source for the claim?)
  emotional_reaction → predicted buyer reaction
}
```

### 9.2.5 Feedback object

```
feedback {
  hypothesis_id        → reference to the finding that produced the hypothesis
  buyer_reaction       → {confirms, partially_confirms, amends, rejects, deflects, competitive_mention, technical_dive, no_urgency}
  reaction_language    → verbatim buyer words (enables CN and MEDDPICC update)
  next_move_triggered  → which branch was taken
  MEDDPICC_fields_updated → list
  rep_note             → freeform
}
```

### 9.2.6 Branch engine

```
branch(reaction_type, role, stage, deal_state) →
  {
    next_exchange_move,
    next_proof_asset,
    next_stakeholder_target,
    MEDDPICC_field_update,
    escalation_flag  → true if two rejections in a row
  }
```

## 9.3 Sequencing rules (encoded in the ruleset)

Rules encoded in PRISM that fire at call-plan generation:

1. **Opening protocol.** Select F1–F6 based on: primary competitor detected × archetype × buyer persona × PRISM confidence.
2. **Hypothesis ranking.** Rank all available hypotheses by (Confidence × Business Impact) ÷ Risk-if-Wrong. Top three become the call plan. Never lead with Low-Confidence × High-Risk.
3. **Reaction routing.** For each hypothesis, pre-generate the four branch paths (confirms / amends / rejects / deflects) with specific deepening questions.
4. **Escalation guardrail.** After two consecutive rejections, flag the call for post-call PRISM data QA. The rep should revert to SPIN.
5. **Buying-committee activation.** At minute 25–35, deploy BC finding + NH. Next-meeting ask must include one stakeholder not volunteered.
6. **Closing protocol.** Deferred Prescription + specific-next-step commit + Champion Narrative handoff.

## 9.5 Five worked end-to-end examples

Each example traces PRISM Finding → Hypothesis → Narrative → Insight Move → Likely Reaction → Follow-up Path → Business Impact. Each compiles to a complete call-plan artifact.

### Example 1 — "Zero Personalization despite Dynamic Yield installed" (Fashion retailer, Merch VP)

- **PRISM Finding (SA + TS):** Site audit shows no detectable personalization on PLPs; BuiltWith/page-source inspection shows Dynamic Yield tags fired but no variant segmentation; two merchandiser job posts open. Confidence: High. Risk-if-wrong: Moderate.
- **Archetype:** Incumbent-displacement (DY in place, under-deployed).
- **Persona:** Merch VP.
- **Competitor detected (CA):** Constructor in evaluation (Finkelshteyn LinkedIn shows case-study request from this retailer's digital team in last 60 days — Confidence: Low for identity specifically).
- **Opening move:** F1 (Diagnostic Preemption).
- **Calibrated Hypothesis (H1):** *"You're paying for Dynamic Yield but your PLPs look identical to me and to a logged-in session I ran with a different purchase history. I think the personalization engine is installed but not wired through to search and category merchandising — which would mean you're paying for capability you're not realizing. I could be wrong about the wiring, but the symptoms match."*
- **Narrative:** You're over-paying for under-deployed personalization — and it's a merch workflow problem, not a vendor problem.
- **Insight move sequence:** F1 → M2 (Calibrated Hypothesis) → M6 (Audit-to-Question Bridge).
- **Likely reactions:**
  - *Confirms* → QS-Deepen: *"How did it get like this — org handoff, vendor implementation, or priority conflict?"* → fills Identified Pain + Champion (who cares about fixing it).
  - *Amends* ("actually it is wired, but only for logged-in traffic") → QS-Reframe: *"That shifts the hypothesis — if only ~25% of traffic is logged-in, you're capturing personalization on a minority. What's that costing on AOV?"* → fills Metrics.
  - *Rejects* → M5 Wounded-Buyer: *"Fair — DY rollouts often stall for reasons that aren't anyone's fault. What's actually running in the personalization layer today?"* → fills Pain while disarming.
- **Follow-up path:** NH — 45-minute working session with merch + digital analytics, Algolia presents a specific A/B design to measure PLP personalization lift against current baseline. Deliverable: draft test plan. Commitment: analytics lead joins.
- **Business Impact framing:** If personalization lift is even 2% of search-driven CVR on PLPs, at their GMV, that's roughly $X–$Y annualized (Falsifiable Benchmark with named assumptions).
- **Champion Narrative for buyer:** *"Algolia mapped our DY deployment gap in 40 minutes of outside analysis — we've been debating it internally for two quarters."*

### Example 2 — "Elevate our customers' omnichannel experience" (Grocery chain, SVP Digital, post-new-CEO 100 days)

- **PRISM Finding (EC + TE + AC):** Q2 earnings call: *"We will elevate our customers' omnichannel experience."* New CEO 100-day mark. Archetype: Platform-consolidation. Hiring: Head of Digital Experience posted 19 days ago.
- **Said-vs-Found gap:** "Omnichannel" framing in earnings; site search returns "bananas" with 420 results and no delivery-window filter.
- **Opening move:** F5 (Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring) — new-CEO era buyers respond to collaborative framing.
- **Calibrated Hypothesis:** *"Your CEO told the Street you're elevating omnichannel, and your site search treats 'milk' identically whether I'm 2 miles from a store with same-day delivery or 200 miles from any location. My guess is your delivery-window metadata is in the catalog but not wired into search ranking. That's a 60-day fix, not a 6-quarter one — but it's invisible to most execs because it doesn't show up in dashboards until you A/B it."*
- **Narrative:** The CEO's narrative is correct; the site search makes him a hypocrite.
- **Insight move sequence:** F5 → M2 → M4 (Competitive Mirror — name a peer grocer that wired delivery-window into search in 2024) → M10 (Deferred Prescription).
- **Likely reactions:**
  - *Confirms* → QS-Deepen on Economic Buyer: *"Who owns omnichannel KPIs in the new org — you, or the new Head of Digital Experience? That shapes what we'd build together."* Fills EB.
  - *Amends* ("we do have delivery filter, just not on initial query") → *"That's an interesting implementation choice — what was the logic? Often that's a query-latency tradeoff."* Fills Pain + Decision Criteria.
  - *Deflects* ("that's not our priority") → QS-Anchor: Falsifiable Benchmark — *"Grocery peers running delivery-wired search report 9–14% conversion lift on fresh/dairy — n≈6, take with salt. Is that worth re-prioritizing?"*
- **Follow-up path:** NH — 30-min with the new Head of Digital Experience once hired; PRISM prepares onboarding dossier as peer-advisor gift.
- **Business Impact framing:** Delivery-wired search adds conversion to the KPI the CEO committed to. The rep is not selling search; the rep is selling the CEO's narrative integrity.
- **Champion Narrative:** *"Algolia showed up with the earnings quote and the live search query pulled up on their screen."*

### Example 3 — Wounded buyer with two failed re-platforms (Fashion DTC brand, Head of Product)

- **PRISM Finding (WB + HS + RG):** LinkedIn archaeology shows two former Directors of Search left within 18 months; GitHub shows abandoned internal-search repository; Glassdoor reviews reference "frustrating search project." Current Head of Product in seat 14 months. Confidence: Medium. Risk-if-wrong: High.
- **Archetype:** Incumbent (failed homegrown / hybrid).
- **Opening move:** F6 (Competitive Mirror) framed softly — do not lead with the wound.
- **Calibrated Hypothesis (with mandatory M9 Documented-Fallibility because Risk-if-wrong is High):** *"I don't know your history, and I could be wrong — but from outside it looks like the team has taken more than one run at replatforming search and has scar tissue about it. Two of the three times that's true when I look at a company, search has a reputation internally as something that never quite works. That might be completely wrong — is it?"*
- **Narrative:** Before any vendor pitch, we need to understand what broke last time so we don't rebuild toward the same failure mode.
- **Insight moves:** M9 → M5 (Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment) → M8 (Status-Returning Question).
- **Likely reactions:**
  - *Confirms* → QS-Deepen: *"Was it relevance quality, merch workflow, or org politics? Each one implies a different approach this time."* Fills Pain + Competition (what didn't work last time).
  - *Amends* ("it wasn't the search project; it was the org restructure") → QS-Reframe: *"So the project gets tagged 'failed' but it's actually a context failure. What would have to be true about the org setup this time?"* Fills Decision Process.
  - *Rejects* → disarm and revert to SPIN: hypothesis was wrong; abandon Exchange for the rest of the call.
- **Follow-up path:** NH — 45-min post-mortem workshop with PRISM's ex-incumbent analysis pre-built. Deliverable: a "what we'd need to be true this time" artifact.
- **Business Impact framing:** The biggest risk of this purchase isn't vendor quality — it's repeating last time's mistake. Structuring the evaluation to de-risk that is worth more than any feature differentiation.
- **Champion Narrative:** *"Algolia named what we all knew but hadn't said out loud — and gave us a way to talk about it without blaming anyone."*

### Example 4 — Greenfield competitive-engineering threat (Public retailer, CTO-led)

- **PRISM Finding (TS + HS + FS):** BuiltWith shows no commerce-search vendor; Elastic detected in log-analysis role; two ML Engineer job posts mentioning "search relevance"; Q3 digital-conversion miss in earnings. Confidence: High. Risk-if-wrong: Low.
- **Archetype:** Greenfield / build-vs-buy.
- **Persona:** CTO (engineering-led).
- **Opening move:** F1 (Diagnostic Preemption) with visible Elastic-unsolved pattern.
- **Calibrated Hypothesis:** *"Your stack suggests you're building search on Elastic in-house. I pulled 20 queries on your site — the typo handling and semantic intent parsing look like what you'd get from Elastic's out-of-box capability, not what a mature in-house team would ship. My guess: the team can build this, but it's not shipping at the pace the CEO needs based on the Q3 miss. I could be wrong about the pace — what's the real roadmap status?"*
- **Narrative:** You have the capability to build this. The question isn't capability; it's opportunity cost.
- **Insight moves:** F1 → M2 → Engineering Opportunity-Cost Mirror → M4 (Competitive Mirror — name a peer that made the same build-vs-buy call).
- **Likely reactions:**
  - *Confirms* → QS-Deepen: *"What else are those engineers not shipping because they're on search?"* Fills Metrics + Identified Pain.
  - *Amends* ("we're not building; we're evaluating") → QS-Reframe on vendor set and timeline.
  - *Rejects* ("our team can ship this") → Engineering Opportunity-Cost Mirror: *"Agree on capability. What's the opportunity cost over the next six months?"*
- **Follow-up path:** NH — technical workshop with SE lead + the CTO's search engineer. Deliverable: build-vs-buy TCO model.
- **Business Impact framing:** Build-vs-buy for commerce search is not about feasibility; it's about which feature the CEO announces this quarter.
- **Champion Narrative:** *"Algolia didn't try to talk us out of Elastic. They helped us do the math on what it would actually cost us not to ship the features the CEO cares about."*

### Example 5 — Trigger event: vacant SVP-Digital role, post-IPO retailer

- **PRISM Finding (TE + BC + FS + AC):** Recent IPO; vacant SVP Digital role (posted 52 days); activist-investor letter citing digital execution; earnings commentary on "digital experience acceleration." Archetype: Platform-consolidation driven by exec-level change.
- **Opening move:** F5 (Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring) — buying-committee ambiguity makes unilateral framing risky.
- **Calibrated Hypothesis:** *"From outside it looks like your digital leadership is in transition — SVP Digital post-IPO, role vacant, activist pressure on digital. My hypothesis is that a search/discovery vendor decision made in the next 3 months will look very different than one made after the SVP hire, because the new hire will want to own the decision. Which means either (a) this deal needs to move faster than the hire, or (b) we should be helping the search for the SVP as much as the search for the vendor. I'm guessing; what's the real dynamic?"*
- **Narrative:** The timing of this decision matters more than the features in it — and Algolia can help you sequence it.
- **Insight moves:** F5 → M2 → M6 (Audit-to-Question Bridge mapped to BC inference) → M10 (Deferred Prescription on waiting for SVP).
- **Likely reactions:**
  - *Confirms* → QS-Deepen on Decision Process: *"Is your CEO willing to make this decision pre-hire, or is that political dynamite?"* Fills Decision Process + Paper Process.
  - *Amends* ("the interim CDO has authority") → *"Does she want to own this — or have the new hire own it? Both are legitimate choices, and they imply different deal paths."* Fills EB.

## 9.6 What PRISM v2 must build

Engineering requirements for the PRISM refactor:

1. **Finding-object schema** — implement per §9.2.1, with all metadata fields mandatory at output time.
2. **Translation layer** — rule engine that takes a finding + context and produces behavior objects.
3. **Confidence scoring** — every finding carries explicit confidence; Low-Confidence × High-Risk findings are gated from rep-facing surfaces.
4. **Call-plan generator** — produces the single-page output per §9.4 for every scheduled call.
5. **Deal-intelligence object** — persists across roles; BDR, AE, SE, CS all read from and write to it.

7. **Hypothesis-quality log** — per-rep dashboard of hypotheses delivered, reactions received, patterns identified.
8. **Outbound-message generator** — micro-Exchange outputs for BDRs (one insight + one calibrated question).
9. **Coaching dashboard** — manager-facing view of call-scoring rubric compliance and MEDDPICC field-quality indicators.
10. **A/B testing infrastructure** — ability to randomize Exchange vs. control at the call level for evidence-tier graduation (🔵 → 🟢).

These ten are the minimum viable spec for PRISM v2. They are the build plan.

# 10. The Training Outline (Skeletal)

**10-second version:** A preliminary structure for how Discovery OS gets taught, not the training content itself. Anchored to adult learning principles, scaffolded for rep tenure, and designed for 90-minute kickoff with 6-month certification path. Full training design belongs to enablement; this section sets the architecture.

## 10.1 Training principles

Rollouts fail when they treat methodology like information transfer. Discovery OS is a behavioral skill; it must be taught as one. Four principles apply:

- **Behavior before theory.** Reps don't adopt methodologies they understand; they adopt methodologies they can execute. Training leads with moves, not paradigm.
- **Scaffolded progression.** Junior reps master 4 moves before all 16. Senior reps get the full library.
- **Deliberate practice in realistic conditions.** Role-play is insufficient. Reps need to run Exchange on real PRISM outputs against simulated buyers before real calls.
- **Coach-to-rubric, not to theory.** Managers coach to the 10-point call-scoring rubric. Rep improves at specific observable behaviors.

## 10.2 The recommended training arc

**Day 1 — Annual Sales Meeting (90 minutes)**
- The thesis (10 min): why the old playbook is obsolete; what changed
- Live Exchange demo (15 min): top AE runs a real first call against a realistic PRISM output
- Core paradigm and the six principles (15 min): the mental model
- The four foundational moves (30 min): M1 Permission Reveal, M2 Calibrated Hypothesis, M6 Audit-to-Question Bridge, M8 Status-Returning Question — deep dive on each
- The single-page call plan (10 min): what PRISM will deliver
- Q&A + skeptical-AE pre-map exposure (10 min)

**Weeks 2–4 — Scaffolded Practice**
- Four 60-minute role-play sessions on the four foundational moves, in pairs
- Each rep runs Exchange on three PRISM-generated call plans against an internal role-player
- Calls recorded and reviewed against the 10-point rubric

**Month 2 — Competitive Pre-Empt Module**
- The 15-objection pre-map
- Competitor-specific first-five-minute strategies
- Role-play against each of the five primary competitor narratives

**Month 3 — Field Execution with Coaching**
- Reps deploy Discovery OS on live first calls
- One call per week per rep reviewed by manager against 10-point rubric
- Weekly cohort debrief on hypothesis-quality patterns

**Month 4–6 — Full-Mode Certification**
- Reps advance to full 16-move library
- Cert criteria: 80%+ 10-point rubric score on 10 consecutive first calls
- Post-cert, rep qualifies for Full Mode call plans (more improvisational, less scripted)

## 10.3 Scaffolded vs. Full Mode

**Scaffolded Mode (months 1–3, or always for reps <12 months tenure):**
- 4 moves only: M1, M2, M6, M8
- 2 opening moves only: F1, F5
- PRISM delivers more pre-scripted language
- Escalation to SPIN after one rejected hypothesis (vs. two in Full Mode)

**Full Mode (post-certification):**
- Full 16-move library
- All 6 opening moves
- PRISM delivers structural guidance, rep improvises language
- Escalation to SPIN after two rejected hypotheses

## 10.4 Coaching architecture

Managers coach via three cadences:

**Weekly.** One-per-rep first-call review against 10-point rubric. 20-minute session per rep.

**Monthly.** Cohort-level hypothesis-quality patterns. Which moves are being executed well? Which are being skipped? Which PRISM outputs are reps ignoring?

**Quarterly.** Methodology-level review with Enablement. What's working? What needs refinement? What should ship to the next version?

## 10.5 What Enablement Must Build

Named explicitly — this is Enablement's scope, not the methodology's scope:

- Day 1 training deck + live demo script
- Role-play scenarios (minimum 12 — three per primary competitor)
- Recorded reference calls (top AE exemplars)
- 10-point rubric scoring guide for managers
- Full-Mode certification exam
- Refresh training materials for quarterly iteration

The methodology hands Enablement the architecture. Enablement builds the content.
