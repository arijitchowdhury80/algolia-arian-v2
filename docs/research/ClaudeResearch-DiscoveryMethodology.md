# PRISM Methodology: Research, Positioning, and Translation Ruleset

**BLUF.** Algolia should replace interrogation-style MEDDPICC discovery with an **insight-led Exchange paradigm** built on a pre-call intelligence platform (PRISM), keeping MEDDPICC as the deal-state checklist while installing Exchange as the conversational engine that actually fills those fields. The primary deliverable below — the **PRISM Translation Ruleset (Section 6)** — is the buildable specification mapping every PRISM finding to executable methodology output. The case rests on three converging facts: (1) 89–94% of B2B buyers now research with LLMs before first calls (Forrester 2024; 6sense 2025, n≈4,000 🟢), so Situation questions insult; (2) peer-reviewed and proprietary conversational-intelligence evidence consistently shows top performers talk ~43% and trade insight for information; (3) Algolia's live landmine — "developer tool vs. Constructor's merchandiser tool" — is pre-planted in most enterprise calls and must be neutralized in the first five minutes before the prospect frames the call around it. The new methodology must succeed in the field under one non-negotiable condition: PRISM-generated hypotheses must be specific, falsifiable, and defensible by a skimming rep without rehearsal.

## 1. The Exchange paradigm

### What Exchange is — and is not

**Exchange** is a discovery mechanism in which the seller enters the first call having already performed the diagnostic work a traditional discovery call would produce, then trades that pre-formed, *falsifiable* interpretation to the buyer in return for correction, texture, and privileged information unavailable externally. The opening posture is "here is what I think is true about your business — tell me where I'm wrong." The instrument of exchange is the **calibrated hypothesis**: specific enough to prove effort, tentative enough to invite disconfirmation.

Mechanically, Exchange borrows from five disciplines that converge on the same behavioral principle:
- **Reciprocity / asymmetric disclosure** — Cialdini 1984; Fehr, Kirchsteiger & Riedl 1993/1998 🟢 (gift-exchange experiments: buyers paid above reservation prices when sellers signaled effort); Collins & Miller 1994 🟢 (meta-analysis, k=94: disclosure lifts liking and reciprocal disclosure).
- **McKinsey/Bain first-meeting protocol** — Minto 1987 🟡 answer-first Pyramid Principle; Rasiel 1999 🟡 hypothesis-driven method; Enns 2010 🟡 peer-to-peer fit assessment; Khalsa & Illig 2008 🟡 "diagnose before prescribe."
- **Clinical diagnostic interviewing** — Baile et al. 2000 🟢 SPIKES (permission-first information delivery); Miller & Rollnick 2013 🟢 motivational interviewing (evocation, rolling with resistance, the righting-reflex problem: telling people what's wrong triggers defense of the status quo).
- **Non-coercive elicitation** — Alison et al. 2013 🟢 ORBIT model (n=418 interviews, structural equation modeling: empathy, autonomy-granting, and adaptive behavior significantly predict interview yield); Voss 2016 🟡 tactical empathy / accusation audit; Dreeke 2017 🟡 Code of Trust (suspend ego, honor reason).
- **Expert advisory practice** — ICF coaching intake protocols; private banking first-meeting norms; medical second-opinion consultations.

**Exchange is NOT:**
- **Not Challenger.** Challenger seeks a "never thought of it that way" moment driven by seller-superior insight (Dixon & Adamson 2011 🟡). Exchange seeks a **correction** — the buyer teaching back. Challenger optimizes for constructive tension and rep control; Exchange optimizes for buyer elaboration.
- **Not a pitch with data dressing.** If the rep narrates conclusions rather than offering them for falsification, it collapses into show-and-tell and triggers the righting-reflex.
- **Not SPIN.** SPIN (Rackham 1988 🟢, n=35,000) sequences Situation→Problem→Implication→Need-payoff *questions*. Exchange inverts the opening: the rep pre-states the implication as hypothesis and asks the buyer to confirm or amend.
- **Not free consulting.** Exchange offers interpretation; remediation stays behind the engagement.

### Core named moves (the mechanics of Exchange)

Each move has a 10-second card and a concrete Algolia first-call example. None are scripts; all are **structural maneuvers** a skimming rep can execute.

**Move 1 — Permission Reveal** 🟢 (SPIKES-I + MI autonomy). Before disclosing pre-call findings, ask explicit consent and frame the deliverable as a hypothesis, not a verdict. *Card: Ask before you tell. Name the work, frame it as hypothesis, give them the veto.* **Algolia example (fashion merch VP):** "Before the demo, I ran ~40 queries on your site search last week and pulled three quotes from your last two earnings calls about conversion. Would it be useful if I walked you through the pattern I think I'm seeing — on the understanding that you have the inside view and I'm probably half-right?"

**Move 2 — Calibrated Hypothesis** 🟡 core / 🟢 MI analog. Single declarative statement: [observed signal] + [mechanical inference] + [tentative business consequence] + [invitation to correct]. Rackham 1988 🟢: flat assertions in major sales correlate with objections, not advances — the tentativeness token is structural, not stylistic. *Card: One hypothesis, named evidence, tentative verb, explicit invitation. "I think X because Y — does that track?"* **Algolia example (grocery product-search owner):** "You added same-day delivery zip codes in Q3 per the press release, and your search still routes 'milk' to the generic PLP before delivery-eligible results. My guess: out-of-stock suppression runs ahead of your delivery-window filter, which would cost roughly the lift same-day was supposed to deliver. I could be wrong about the sequence — how close is that?"

**Move 3 — Peer-Status Anchor** 🟡 (Enns; Khalsa). In the first two minutes, frame the meeting as peer-to-peer fit assessment, not vendor audition. Give the buyer an early real "out." *Card: Open with "let's see if we fit," not "let me show you what we do." Give them the out.* **Algolia example:** "My job for the next 30 minutes isn't to pitch Algolia — it's to figure out with you whether what we do maps to what's breaking for your team. If 15 minutes in we both think it's not a fit, I'd rather we say so than run the full meeting."

**Move 4 — Competitive Mirror** 🟡 (CEB). Name what a specific peer does differently without editorializing on who is "ahead"; ask the buyer to attribute motive. *Card: Name what a specific peer is doing. Don't rank. Ask them to explain the gap.* **Algolia example:** "Sephora moved their product-attribute query parsing to vector+keyword hybrid about 18 months ago — you can see it in how 'red lipstick matte drugstore-dupe' resolves on their site vs. yours. I don't know if that's right for your margin structure. How do you think about that tradeoff for your category?"

**Move 5 — Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment** 🟢 (Voss accusation audit; MI empathy; ORBIT). Label the likely prior scar without claiming to know their history; underclaim specifics; pause. *Card: Name the scar they didn't mention. Underclaim. Pause.* **Algolia example:** "My hunch is the last time someone tried to fix relevance, it turned into a nine-month roadmap item that got deprioritized when Black Friday came — and now 'search' has a reputation internally as something that never quite works. That might be totally off; something like that is pretty common at this stage."

**Move 6 — Audit-to-Question Bridge** 🟢 (SPIKES S→P→K→E). Pair every external finding with an internal-context question only the buyer can answer. *Card: Every external finding buys one internal question. Spend the coin.* **Algolia example:** "Site-wide, about 17% of sessions end on a zero-results page for queries with three or more tokens — that's the external view. What I can't see: does that show up in your team's week? Is it surfaced by merch, by e-comm, or is it invisible until QBR?"

**Move 7 — Falsifiable Benchmark** 🟡/🔵. Offer a quantitative anchor with the uncertainty band *explicitly named*. The error bar is the reciprocity gift. *Card: Anchor a number with an explicit error bar. The uncertainty IS the gift.* **Algolia example:** "In fashion-retail benchmark cohorts I've seen (n≈20, mid-market mix, take with salt), search-driven revenue runs 22–38% of total digital. Your public numbers suggest low end. I'm inferring from traffic-mix proxies, not your real attribution — right zip code?"

**Move 8 — Status-Returning Question** 🟢 (MI/OARS + ORBIT autonomy). After the rep delivers content, immediately hand epistemic status back. Budget one status-returner per 60 seconds of rep talk. *Card: After you teach, ask. Hand status back. One question per minute of rep talk.* **Algolia example:** "That's the outside view. You've run merch here four years — what does that pattern look like from where you sit? I'm probably missing two or three things that would reframe it."

**Move 9 — Documented-Fallibility Disclosure** 🟢 (Collins & Miller vulnerability effect). Proactively name one specific thing the rep's research couldn't see. *Card: Name one thing you couldn't see. Specific, not generic. Once.* **Algolia example:** "One thing I genuinely can't tell from outside: whether your search team reports into merch, product, or engineering. That completely changes which hypothesis I'd lead with — it's my first question."

**Move 10 — Deferred Prescription** 🟡 (Enns; Khalsa; medical second-opinion). Refuse to recommend a solution in the first call even when invited. *Card: Refuse to prescribe in call one. Name what you'd need to know. Book the next call on those three things.* **Algolia example:** "I could guess at a recommendation, but I'd want your top-50 zero-result queries, your attribution model for search-influenced revenue, and 15 minutes with whoever owns merch taxonomy. A recommendation today is probably half-right, and half-right recommendations are how these projects die in month four."

Plus six competitive-narrative moves — **Diagnostic Preemption, Category Redefinition, False-Binary Collapse, Cost-of-Engineering Audit, Consolidation-Risk Surfacing, Silent-Incumbent Reveal** — catalogued in Section 4.

### Where Exchange works and where it fails (honest)

**Works when:** senior operators who can distinguish calibrated hypothesis from pitch; first call through early discovery; rep has ≥12 months category depth (or is paired with an SE who does); PRISM output has been critically reviewed, not just skimmed; performance-pressured buyer organization with autonomy to act.

**Fails when (specific):**
- **Low-information domains** — PRISM returns generic output, the "gift" fails Fehr's effort-signaling condition, reciprocity doesn't fire. Revert to SPIN.
- **Procurement-led / regulated buyers** (healthcare, financial services, public sector) — trained to neutralize asymmetric-information plays; Exchange reads as manipulation.
- **Face-saving cultures** — naming an unspoken problem creates loss of face; Wounded-Buyer and Calibrated Hypothesis are especially risky. Let the buyer surface first; reflect in pure MI mode.
- **Rep lacking domain depth** — the single largest failure. A rep who cannot defend the *mechanism* behind a hypothesis when asked "why do you think that?" gets exposed in 90 seconds, and the more effort PRISM put in, the worse the contrast.
- **PRISM data wrong** — confidently framing hypotheses off bad data (wrong exec quote, outdated tech stack, misread earnings call) is near-unrecoverable. Documented-Fallibility Disclosure must be mandatory at the methodology level; pre-call QA of PRISM output must be non-negotiable.
- **Hostile buyer** — forced into the meeting, favored competitor, team loses budget if the project proceeds. ORBIT 🟢 is explicit: with committed non-engagers, rapport mechanics yield little. Use a short respectful disqualification check.
- **Early-career buyer demonstrating process** — a Director new-in-seat often needs a visibly rigorous multi-vendor evaluation for internal credibility. Exchange compresses discovery into a peer conversation, threatening the political narrative they need to tell. **Solution:** honor the process; run SPIN-style discovery *even when* you know the answers.
- **Ambiguous signal / non-unique hypothesis** — if the hypothesis is true for 80% of the category, no "exchange" happens; nothing is owed.

### When is question-led actually better?

Rackham's original research remains the most honest constraint on Exchange: top performers in major sales asked *fewer* Situation questions but *4× more* Implication questions (Rackham 1988 🟢, n=35,000 calls, 12-year longitudinal). Rackham did **not** claim hypothesis-sharing outperforms question-asking. He claimed *better questions* outperform average questions, and that Situation questions are now better answered by pre-call research. SPIN is better than Exchange when: (1) the buyer has not yet recognized the problem (self-generated change talk predicts behavior change — Miller & Rollnick 🟢 — better than externally imposed framing); (2) rep domain depth is below the buyer's; (3) procurement/committee requires documented requirements-elicitation; (4) early-career buyers need to demonstrate rigor; (5) cultures where asserting is presumptuous. **The honest synthesis: Exchange is a compression of SPIN's Situation and early-Problem phases into a single hypothesis-trade move that earns the right to ask higher-quality Implication and Need-Payoff questions faster. A rep who cannot follow a Calibrated Hypothesis with excellent Implication questioning will be worse than a pure SPIN rep.**

Enablement must measure whether the invitation-to-correct was extended, not just whether the hypothesis was delivered. 🔵

### Deep-dive on MEDDPICC

**What MEDDPICC does well** (🟡 practitioner consensus). It is the single most widely adopted enterprise-software qualification checklist. It gives sales leaders a forensic lens on deal health: Metrics (quantified pain), Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identified Pain, Champion, Competition. It exposes skipped steps — the reason it won at PTC under Dunkel/Napoli is that it makes missing information visible to deal reviewers.

**What MEDDPICC is silent on.** It says *nothing* about how to earn the information. It assumes the rep can elicit EB identity, decision criteria, and quantified pain — but every methodology that claims to do this (SPIN, Challenger, Sandler, Solution Selling) is a separate bolted-on conversational engine. In Algolia's current practice, the engine is "persona-specific discovery question banks" — which in the AI era means asking a merch VP questions the merch VP already answered in ChatGPT before the call.

**Why this fails in the first 5 minutes.** 73% of B2B buyers avoid reps with irrelevant outreach (Gartner Sales Survey 2025 🟢). 89–94% use LLMs during evaluation (Forrester 2024; 6sense 2025, n≈4,000 🟢). 82% prioritize credibility over likability (Sales Growth Company 2025 🟡). A rep opening a discovery call with "tell me about your business" signals they are less prepared than the LLM the buyer already consulted. The MEDDPICC question bank compounds the damage — it surfaces as interrogation at exactly the moment the buyer expects peer dialog.

### Positioning statement (1-page)

**MEDDPICC tells you what to qualify. PRISM Methodology tells you how to earn that information in a world where interrogation destroys trust.**

MEDDPICC is the deal-health checklist — the canonical list of fields every enterprise deal must carry: Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identified Pain, Champion, Competition. Algolia is keeping MEDDPICC. Nothing in this methodology replaces it.

What MEDDPICC has never had is a conversational engine fit for the 2024–2026 buyer. Its field-filling assumed a buyer who hadn't yet evaluated the category. That buyer is extinct. Today's prospect arrives having researched Algolia, Constructor, Coveo, and Bloomreach in ChatGPT and Perplexity, having already scored 85% of their decision criteria (6sense 2024 🟢), and having already placed a preferred vendor on the Day One shortlist 94% of the time (6sense 2025 🟢). In that context, a rep running a MEDDPICC question bank sounds like someone cramming for an exam the buyer already passed.

**PRISM Methodology is the engine.** PRISM assembles the site audit, exec commentary, tech stack, and financial signals a rep would previously have spent discovery calls extracting. Exchange turns those findings into calibrated hypotheses the buyer corrects — and every correction fills a MEDDPICC field with language the buyer *owns*, not language the rep extracted. Champions emerge from reciprocity, not qualification. Pain gets stated in the buyer's KPIs because the rep already staked a hypothesis on them. The EB gets identified when the Audit-to-Question Bridge asks "whose number moves if we fix this" — a question that feels like sensemaking, not interrogation.

**Internal objection pre-empt.** "We already have MEDDPICC" — yes, and 60% of our reps have never filled in Metrics with quantified numbers the customer confirmed. That's not a MEDDPICC failure. It's a conversational-engine failure. PRISM Methodology fixes the upstream problem: it gives reps a pre-call diagnostic that makes MEDDPICC fields *want* to fill themselves through natural dialog, rather than through questions that audit how much the rep failed to research.

### Keep / Replace / Augment for Algolia's current practice

- **KEEP:** MEDDPICC as deal-state model. Persona awareness (merch/product/eng/exec) as the vocabulary layer. Call-plan discipline. Deal reviews checking MEDDPICC completeness.
- **REPLACE:** Persona discovery question banks used as opening interrogation → Exchange moves as opening protocol. "Tell me about your business" → Calibrated Hypothesis. BANT-style qualification in Q1 of first call → buying-network mapping in Q2-Q3.
- **AUGMENT:** Every MEDDPICC field now has a "PRISM pre-fill" and an "Exchange earn" — what PRISM populates pre-call, what the rep confirms through reciprocal dialog. Champion development gets paired with Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment; Competition gets paired with Competitive Mirror and the Silent-Incumbent Reveal.

## 3. Quantitative discovery-call research synthesis

The 15–20 strongest evidence-based claims about what correlates with closed-won at the discovery-call level, with direct Algolia AE translations. Most of this data is 🟡 — proprietary to Gong, Chorus, CEB/Gartner — because peer-reviewed sales research at this grain does not yet exist. Where I mark 🟢 I am citing peer-reviewed work or very large primary studies; where I mark 🟡 I flag vendor-adjacent data; where 🔵, I am transposing from adjacent disciplines.

### The credibility anchor — 17 claims

**Claim 1 — Talk/listen ratio.** Top-performing AEs in B2B software maintain roughly **43:57 rep:buyer** talk-time in discovery, vs. ~65:35 for average reps (Gong Labs analysis, n≈519,000 calls, 2016–2019). 🟡 Caveat: Gong's customer base skews mid-market B2B SaaS.
**AE translation:** In a 45-minute first call with a merch VP, target ~19 minutes of rep talk. Use PRISM hypothesis statements (30–60 seconds each) to trigger buyer reaction, then listen. Do NOT fill the 3-second post-hypothesis silence — that's where priority reveals itself.

**Claim 2 — Question count.** Winning B2B discovery calls contain **11–14 buyer-facing questions**, distributed across the call rather than clustered early (Gong Labs, n≈519K). 🟡
**AE translation:** Budget ~3 questions per 10 minutes. Do not front-load. Use Audit-to-Question Bridge so every question follows an insight the rep earned with.

**Claim 3 — Open/closed mix.** Discovery calls that advance use ~70% open-ended questions; closed-ending heavy calls correlate with lost deals (Gong; Chorus). 🟡
**AE translation:** After PRISM hypothesis delivery, the primary follow-up should be open — "how close is that?" not "is that right?"

**Claim 4 — Monologue threshold.** Continuous rep talk segments over ~150 seconds correlate strongly with lost discovery calls; top performers cap at ~76 seconds (Gong Labs). 🟡
**AE translation:** If you're still talking at 90 seconds, you've become a pitch. Cut. Hand back with Status-Returning Question.

**Claim 5 — Pricing timing.** Discovery calls that surface pricing after minute 38–46 correlate with closed-won; calls that introduce pricing in the first 15 minutes correlate with stalled deals (Gong; Chorus). 🟡
**AE translation:** If the buyer asks pricing in the opening, use Deferred Prescription — "I could give you a range now, but the range I'd give without knowing your query volume and catalog specifics would be wrong in a way that costs you credibility upstairs."

**Claim 6 — Multithreading.** Deals with ≥4 stakeholders engaged by the supplier during evaluation close at approximately 2× the rate of single-threaded deals; mean B2B buying-group size is now 11–13 (Forrester 2024 🟢; 6sense 2024 🟢).
**AE translation:** Before the first call, PRISM should surface buying-committee inference. After the first call, the rep's next-meeting ask should include at least one additional stakeholder name the buyer did not volunteer — from PRISM's LinkedIn/hiring-signal data.

**Claim 7 — Pre-call research depth.** Reps whose first-call agenda references specific account signals (earnings-call quotes, recent hires, tech-stack changes) advance to second meeting at significantly higher rates than reps delivering generic overviews (directionally ~30-50% higher second-meeting rate across Gong, Outreach, Spotlight.ai practitioner research 🟡; not peer-reviewed).
**AE translation:** The first 90 seconds of every first call must reference at least one PRISM finding by name — earnings quote, job post, site-audit gap.

**Claim 8 — Specific next-step commitment.** Discovery calls ending with a specific calendar-booked next step (date, attendees, agenda) convert to closed-won at 3–4× the rate of calls ending with "let's circle back" (Gong Labs 🟡; HubSpot 2023 🟡).
**AE translation:** Do not end any call without booking the next one on the call. PRISM's next-meeting-hook field must be spoken aloud before minute 40.

**Claim 9 — Insight delivery.** Buyers report "I learned something valuable" in 53% of supplier interactions in high-loyalty accounts vs. 21% in low-loyalty (CEB Challenger research 🟡; CEB-internal data, pre-Gartner). Sensemaking sellers who help buyers prioritize conflicting information drive 2.8× higher likelihood of purchase ease and 3× higher likelihood of a larger, lower-regret deal (Gartner 2019+ 🟢).
**AE translation:** Every first call should contain one "teachable moment" the buyer could not have gotten from a Perplexity query — peer-match data, tradeoff framing, or PRISM's competitive capability matrix.

**Claim 10 — Linguistic certainty.** Reps using hedging language ("kind of," "maybe," "I think we might") correlate with lower outcome rates than reps using calibrated certainty; BUT over-confident assertion ("absolutely," "definitely") correlates with buyer skepticism (Chorus.ai linguistic analysis 🟡).
**AE translation:** Calibrated Hypothesis language = hedged framing + specific evidence. "I could be wrong about the sequence, but the public data suggests X" — not "I think maybe X" and not "X is definitely happening."

**Claim 11 — "We" vs. "you" pronoun use.** Higher "we" frequency in later-stage deals correlates with closed-won; "we" in first discovery is premature and signals overreach (Gong Labs 🟡).
**AE translation:** Stay in "you/your" language for first call. Shift to "we" only after the buyer has said something like "so how would we approach this."

**Claim 12 — Silence after question.** Reps who hold silence >3 seconds after open-ended questions elicit materially longer and more substantive buyer responses (Chorus 🟡; consistent with Alison ORBIT research on pause-and-hold 🟢).
**AE translation:** After delivering a Calibrated Hypothesis, count to 5 before speaking again. Most AEs re-explain at 2 seconds and lose the reciprocity window.

**Claim 13 — Pricing-discussion precision.** Deals where the rep names a specific dollar range with stated assumptions (rather than "it depends on your usage") advance faster and at higher close rates (Gong 🟡; Corporate Visions research 🟡).
**AE translation:** When forced into pricing, use Falsifiable Benchmark: "For a retailer at roughly your query volume and catalog size, our pricing tends to fall in the $X–$Y range — that assumes Z. If your numbers are materially different from Z, the range shifts."

**Claim 14 — Competitor mention.** Deals where the rep proactively names 2–3 competitors with honest acknowledgment of specific strengths close at higher rates than deals where the rep avoids the competitive frame until forced (Gong 🟡; consistent with Cialdini's two-sided messaging literature 🟢).
**AE translation:** Competitive Mirror + honest-tradeoff disclosure within first 10 minutes. "You're likely evaluating us against Constructor and Coveo. Honest take: if X, Constructor is serious; if Y, Coveo is serious; where Algolia wins is Z."

**Claim 15 — Regret and sensemaking.** 60% of B2B tech buyers regret a recent purchase; only 17% of deals qualify as "high-quality, low-regret"; hybrid digital+human buying experiences are 1.8× more likely to be high-quality than either alone (Gartner 🟢; n=3,484 Gartner Digital Markets 2024).
**AE translation:** Lead with tradeoff framing, not feature pitch. Name where Algolia is *wrong* for their context — this is the signal sensemaking buyers weight most heavily.

**Claim 16 — Buyer priority on credibility over likability.** 82% of B2B buyers weight credibility above likability in vendor selection (Sales Growth Company 2025 🟡); 60% say reps don't uncover the real business problem.
**AE translation:** Demonstrate credibility through specific, data-anchored hypothesis in the first 90 seconds. Warmth follows credibility; the reverse does not hold in 2026.

**Claim 17 — Dark-funnel arrival.** 85% of buyers have established evaluation criteria before first contact; 94% of buying groups have ranked a preferred vendor pre-contact; only 5–6% of buying time is spent with any one vendor when multiple are compared (6sense 2024–2025 🟢; Gartner B2B Buying Journey 🟢).
**AE translation:** Within first 10 minutes, deploy the Silent-Incumbent Reveal: "Are we your current front-runner, a credible #2, or are we on the list because procurement made you get three bids? I'll structure this call differently depending." Buyers nearly always answer honestly to this frame.

### Skeptical-AE objection pre-map

1. **"Gong data is biased toward Gong customers — mostly mid-market SaaS, not enterprise commerce search."** Honest response: True. Treat all Gong claims as directional, not prescriptive. But the underlying mechanisms (reciprocity, tactical empathy, sensemaking) are validated in peer-reviewed work from psychology (Collins & Miller 1994 🟢), interrogation research (Alison ORBIT 🟢), and B2B buying research (Gartner 🟢) that is *not* vendor-adjacent.
2. **"Averages don't apply to complex enterprise deals."** Right — averages show you patterns, not play-calls. That's why Exchange is a framework of named moves, not a script. Reps adapt.
3. **"Talk-ratio research is cherry-picked — my top deals have high rep talk-time because customers asked me to teach them."** Correct for a subset. Exchange's 43:57 target applies to *discovery*; demos and mid-funnel technical deep-dives legitimately flip the ratio.
4. **"Questions count rules ignore the kind of deal."** Correct. Transactional deals need fewer; strategic enterprise deals need more. Use these as first-call discovery baselines, not universal.
5. **"Gartner / 6sense are analyst firms selling to vendors — their 'buyers prefer X' data is survey-based, not behaviorally measured."** True. Flag every such stat as self-report. Where possible, pair with behavioral data (Gong/Chorus).
6. **"This is all 2022 research, and AI has changed everything."** Partially correct for some claims (Situation-question penalty has intensified; dark-funnel stats have moved). Cited 2024–2026 data wherever available. Where evidence is still forming (AI-buyer trust destruction mechanism), explicitly labeled 🔵.
7. **"I already do most of this."** Fine. Exchange is a forcing function, not a revolution. The forcing function matters because 60% of peers don't.

### First-five-minute moves (highest-leverage)

Six named moves designed to pre-empt competitive framing in the call opening, before the prospect raises the objection.

**Move F1 — Diagnostic Preemption** 🔵 (McKinsey hypothesis-first meeting protocol + Cialdini reciprocity + Burgoon expectancy-violation). Open without self-introduction; share three PRISM findings — one relevance gap, one merchandising gap, one revenue-capture gap — and invite pushback *before* anything else. **Card:** *Arrive with three hypotheses. Ask for pushback before anything else.* **Example:** "Sarah, before we begin — I'll skip the intro and share three hypotheses I formed from your site last night. (1) Your 'black dress' search returns 847 results, and 12 of the first 20 are out-of-stock — I suspect OOS burial requires a dev ticket. (2) Your zero-result rate on brand searches is ~4%, three times industry — usually a PIM synonym issue. (3) Your search-driven revenue share is likely 35–40% of digital, but you have no A/B framework to prove incremental lift. If any are wrong, I want to know. If right, let's spend the call on those three problems — not a demo."

**Move F2 — Category Redefinition** 🔵 (April Dunford category design). Never say "we're a search platform." Name a broader category — *discovery infrastructure for AI-era commerce* — and evaluate competitors inside that frame. **Card:** *Name a bigger category than "commerce search" — before they put you in the smaller one.* **Example:** "Quick framing note: 'commerce search' is about to stop mattering. Shoppers arrive via ChatGPT, agentic checkout, and native app surfaces — not just site search. The category that matters is *discovery infrastructure*: a composable relevance layer serving your site, apps, agents, and AI surfaces from one index. Constructor, Bloomreach, Coveo are excellent at the old category. What I want to spend this call on is whether the new category matters to your 18-month roadmap."

**Move F3 — Merchandiser Permission Reveal** 🔵 (SPIKES + Brehm reactance theory). Name the narrative the prospect has likely been told ("Algolia is a dev tool"), ask permission to challenge it, then challenge with a live demonstration — not a slide. **Card:** *Name the narrative, ask permission, then challenge with evidence not rhetoric.*

**Move F4 — Peer-Status Anchor** 🔵 (Cialdini social proof — peer-segment). Open with 2–3 named Algolia customers matching the prospect's industry, revenue, catalog, stack — before they ask for references. **Card:** *Open with a named peer before they ask for references.*

**Move F5 — Shared-Agenda Co-Authoring** 🔵 (Schein humble inquiry + Ben Franklin effect). Share a PRISM-grounded draft agenda on screen; invite edits; treat edits as binding. **Card:** *Give them a draft agenda and ask them to edit it.*

**Move F6 — Competitive Mirror** 🔵 (Cialdini pre-suasion / two-sided messaging). Name 2–3 competitors in the opening with specific strengths acknowledged, then name Algolia's one material advantage. **Card:** *Name each competitor's real strength before you name yours.*

### Competitor-specific pre-empt strategies

## 5. The AI-era buying context

### The collapse of information asymmetry on both sides

The 2022 B2B discovery playbook assumed the rep knew more about the vendor's capability than the buyer did, and the buyer knew more about their own problem than the rep did. Both halves of that assumption are now broken.

**On the buyer side:**
- **89–94%** of B2B buyers use LLMs during their buying process (Forrester 2024 89%; 6sense 2025 94%, n≈4,000 global buyers 🟢).
- **95%** anticipate using genAI in purchase decisions within 12 months (Forrester State of Business Buying 2024 🟢).
- **72%** encounter Google AI Overviews, and **90% of those** click through to cited sources — meaning buyers triangulate LLM claims against primary sources (TrustRadius 2025 🟢).
- **62%** of frequent AI users "always or very often fact-check" vendor claims — AI-heavy buyers are *more* skeptical, not less (TrustRadius 2025 🟢).

**On the rep side (if Algolia builds PRISM well):**
- Full site search audit with 10-dimension scoring — the rep arrives knowing the buyer's live search failure modes.
- Exec earnings-call quotes and "said-vs-found" analysis.
- Tech stack inference (BuiltWith, SimilarTech, job posts).
- Financial signals and trigger events.
- Competitive capability matrix pre-populated.

### How this changes first-call expectations

- **86%** of B2B customers expect companies to be well-informed about them at interaction (Gartner n=5,800+ 🟢).
- **59%** say reps don't take time to understand their business (Salesforce State of Sales 2024 🟢).
- **73%** avoid sellers who send irrelevant outreach (Gartner Sales Survey 2025 🟢).
- **82%** prioritize credibility over likability (Sales Growth Company 2025 🟡).
- **67%** rank discovery as the most important part of the sales process (Sales Growth Company 🟡).
- **85%** have established purchase requirements before first contact; **94%** of buying groups have ranked a preferred vendor pre-contact (6sense 2024–2025 🟢).
- Only **5–6%** of buying time is spent with any one vendor when multiple are compared (Gartner B2B Buying Journey 🟢).

**Does asking questions the buyer already answered in their own Perplexity research destroy trust?** Evidence is strong but indirect. We have: (a) 90%+ of buyers expect reps to know their business; (b) 73% avoid reps with irrelevant outreach; (c) Gong data showing generic-question-heavy openings correlate with lower advance rates. We do *not* have a published RCT isolating "pre-answered question → measured trust drop." The inference is robust enough for methodology design 🟡 but the causal mechanism is 🔵.

### Dark-funnel behavior and vendor differentiation under collapsed asymmetry

The often-cited "57% of buying decision before sales contact" (CEB 2015 🟢) has been superseded. Current Gartner framing: 17% of buying time with *all* suppliers combined; 5–6% per vendor when multiple are compared. 6sense 2024: ~70% of journey before engaging sellers. 6sense 2025: Point of First Contact shifted from 69%→61% of journey (buyers contacting sellers 6-7 weeks earlier) but 95% of winning vendors are on Day One shortlist and 77-80% of deals go to the pre-contact favorite.

**The new basis of rep value** when both sides have consulting-grade intelligence:
1. **Sensemaking / tradeoff judgment.** Gartner 🟢: customers whose suppliers help navigate conflicting info are 2.8× more likely to experience purchase ease and 3× more likely to buy a larger, lower-regret deal.
2. **Peer-match insight the LLM cannot produce.** 56% of buyers consult existing product users before purchase (71% for enterprise); 72% hire consultants/analysts (6sense 2024; TrustRadius 2024 🟢).
3. **Novel framing.** Buyers arrive overconfident from AI research; the rep's job is to legitimately reframe, not re-explain.
4. **Honest tradeoff disclosure.** 49% of buyers say the #1 thing they'd change is transparent pricing/tradeoff info (TrustRadius 2025 🟢). Reps who explicitly say "here's where we lose" build trust LLMs cannot fake.

### 2022 vs. 2026 first-call playbook (explicit contrast)

| 2022 | 2026 AI-era |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about your business" | Specific, research-backed hypothesis about their commerce KPIs |
| BANT qualification | Map buying network: "who needs to believe this?" |
| Pitch differentiators | Name where Algolia is the wrong choice first |
| Save peer stories for mid-funnel | Lead with peer match: "three retailers your size decided X/Y/Z" |
| Try to make Algolia the preferred vendor on call one | Ask where you rank; tailor accordingly |
| 50/50 talk ratio | ~43/57, trade insight for information |
| Treat call as information gathering | Treat call as sensemaking + tradeoff arbitration |
| Ask what buyer already told ChatGPT | Ask only what ChatGPT couldn't answer |

### Honest gaps in the AI-era evidence

- Controlled studies of first-call opening scripts in the AI-buyer era do not yet exist. Most evidence is practitioner (🟡) or inferred (🔵).
- Causality between AI research use and trust destruction is inferred from two robust facts, not measured directly.
- Gartner "75% prefer rep-free" shifted to 67% (Mar 2026) — treat as contested, not settled.
- B2C-commerce-buyer-buying-B2B-tech specifically is under-studied; the general B2B patterns likely apply with commerce-KPI anchoring.
- PRISM-specific outcome claims are untestable externally. Algolia should instrument A/B measurement on Exchange vs. control within its own funnel to move claims from 🔵 to 🟢.

## 6. The PRISM Translation Ruleset (PRIMARY DELIVERABLE)

This is the foundational translation specification. It is comprehensive enough to be built against: a schema for PRISM findings, a schema for methodology outputs, metadata that every output must carry, sequencing logic that encodes when one exchange move triggers another, and five worked end-to-end examples. Build against this.

### 6.1 PRISM finding categories

Every PRISM output is tagged with one or more of these finding categories. This is the input vocabulary of the ruleset.

| Code | Finding category | Source example |
|---|---|---|
| **SA** | Search audit gap (critical/moderate) | "Zero Personalization despite Dynamic Yield installed" |
| **EC** | Executive commentary | Earnings-call quote: "elevate our customers' omnichannel experience" |
| **TS** | Tech stack signal | BuiltWith shows Algolia not deployed; Coveo Service Cloud present |
| **HS** | Hiring signal | Job post: "Senior Merchandiser, Onsite Search" open 47 days |
| **FS** | Financial signal | Q3 miss on digital conversion; guidance cut; activist investor |
| **CA** | Competitive action | Incumbent vendor renewal window; PR release on competitor adoption |
| **BC** | Buying-committee inference | SVP Digital vacant; new CDO started 3 months ago |
| **WB** | Wounded-buyer pattern | Two failed search re-platform attempts (inferred from LinkedIn + GitHub archaeology) |
| **TE** | Trigger event | New-CEO first 100 days; post-IPO; M&A close |
| **AC** | Archetype classification | Greenfield / Incumbent-displacement / Platform consolidation / Composable migration |

**Proposed additions 🔵:**
- **BL** — Brand-language gap: PRISM-inferred mismatch between how execs talk about customer experience and what the site behavior delivers (fuel for Said-vs-Found).
- **RG** — Regret signal: G2/Reddit/LinkedIn evidence of public customer dissatisfaction with incumbent.
- **CM** — Category-momentum signal: peer-retailer adoption patterns in last 6 months.

### 6.2 Methodology output types per finding

For each finding, PRISM produces one or more of these outputs. This is the output vocabulary.

| Output type | Description |
|---|---|
| **H** — Hypothesis statement | A single declarative, falsifiable claim about the buyer's business ready for Calibrated Hypothesis delivery |
| **EM** — Exchange-move script (structural) | Which named move (F1–F6, M1–M10) to deploy, with structural guidance |
| **QS** — Question stack | Ordered follow-ups to deploy after the buyer responds — specifically designed to fill MEDDPICC fields through reciprocal dialog |
| **OP** — Objection pre-map | The 1–3 most likely objections this finding will surface, each with named response pattern |
| **CN** — Champion narrative | A narrative the buyer can carry internally to a skeptical stakeholder, in their stakeholder's language |
| **OE** — Outbound email angle | Pre-meeting outreach angle to shape expectations |
| **NH** — Next-meeting hook | Specific, calendar-able next-meeting framing |
| **DT** — Disclosure timing guidance | When in the call to deploy the finding (opening 5 min / discovery arc / after 25-minute mark / reserved for follow-up) |

### 6.3 Metadata every output must carry

Every PRISM-generated output in every rep-facing view must include:

| Metadata field | Purpose | Values |
|---|---|---|
| **Confidence** | How sure PRISM is of the underlying signal | High / Medium / Low — Low = gate with Documented-Fallibility Disclosure |
| **Archetype-sensitivity** | Which archetypes this output applies to | Greenfield / Incumbent-displacement / Platform consolidation / Composable migration / all |
| **Persona-sensitivity** | Which persona this is tuned for | Merch / Product / Eng / Exec |
| **Evidence grounding** | What specific data PRISM used | URL / quote / metric with timestamp |
| **Risk-if-wrong** | What happens if the hypothesis is incorrect | Low / Moderate / High — High = demands Documented-Fallibility Disclosure in delivery |
| **Evidence tier of mechanism** | 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔵 for the *move* being triggered | Feeds enablement |
| **Deploy / do-not-deploy flags** | Culture, buyer seniority, archetype exclusions | e.g., do-not-deploy if buyer seniority < Director AND archetype = Incumbent-displacement |

### 6.4 Sequencing logic

PRISM outputs are not delivered in parallel; they are chained. The ruleset encodes the conversation arc: **Data → Signal → Hypothesis → Prioritization → Narrative → Insight → Reaction → Follow-up → Business Impact.**

Key sequencing rules:

1. **Opening protocol (0–5 minutes).** Deploy exactly one of F1–F6 based on: primary competitor detected (from TS+CA findings), archetype, and buyer persona. If Constructor is detected as primary competitor → F3 (Merchandiser Permission Reveal) is default unless buyer is engineering-led. If Coveo → F2 (Category Redefinition). If homegrown → F1 with an Elastic-unsolved diagnostic pattern.

2. **Hypothesis delivery (5–12 minutes).** Three Calibrated Hypotheses max, each from a different finding category (e.g., one SA, one EC, one TS). Rank by Confidence × Business Impact. Never lead with Low-Confidence or High-Risk-if-Wrong.

3. **Reaction classification (12–20 minutes).** Buyer response classified as: *confirms* / *amends* / *rejects* / *deflects*. Each branch triggers a different QS:
   - **Confirms →** QS-Deepen (how bad is it, who knows, what's tried, what's the business cost)
   - **Amends →** QS-Reframe (take the amendment as new data; re-ask the hypothesis in buyer-owned language)
   - **Rejects →** QS-Disarm (Wounded-Buyer Acknowledgment + Status-Returning Question; do not argue)
   - **Deflects →** QS-Anchor (Falsifiable Benchmark to re-anchor with a second data point)

4. **Escalation triggers.** If two consecutive hypotheses are rejected, abandon Exchange for this call and revert to SPIN-style discovery — the rep is out of domain depth or PRISM data is miscalibrated. This is a guardrail, not a failure.

5. **Buying-committee activation (25–35 minutes).** Deploy BC finding + NH. Next-meeting ask must include at least one stakeholder the buyer did not volunteer.

6. **Closing protocol (last 5 minutes).** Deferred Prescription + specific-next-step commit + Champion Narrative handoff.

### 6.6 Branch logic summary (what the ruleset compiles to)

Every PRISM finding compiles to a call-flow artifact containing:
1. **Opening move** (F1–F6) selected by archetype × competitor-detected × persona.
2. **Three ranked hypotheses** with metadata (Confidence, Risk-if-wrong, Evidence grounding). High-Confidence × Low-Risk hypotheses lead.
3. **Branch tree for buyer reaction** — confirms/amends/rejects/deflects, each with specific QS designed to fill named MEDDPICC fields.
4. **Escalation guardrail** — two rejected hypotheses → revert to SPIN; flag for post-call PRISM data QA.
5. **Buying-committee activation trigger** — at minute 25–35, deploy BC + NH.
6. **Closing protocol** — Deferred Prescription + next-step commit + Champion Narrative handoff.

Every artifact is single-page, scannable, and terminates in something executable without rehearsal. No concept ships without (1) full explanation + evidence, (2) 10-second skim version, (3) concrete Algolia in-call example.

## 7. Final synthesis: the six questions answered

**What exactly are we replacing in old Algolia discovery?** The MEDDPICC persona-specific question banks used as the *conversational engine* of first-call discovery. Not MEDDPICC itself. The interrogation model collapsed because 89–94% of B2B buyers now arrive having researched Algolia with LLMs (Forrester 2024; 6sense 2025 🟢), and Situation-type questions in that context signal the rep is less prepared than the buyer's AI.

**What exactly are we keeping and augmenting?** MEDDPICC as the deal-state model and review lens. Persona awareness as a vocabulary layer (merch/product/eng/exec speak differently; the core methodology is universal). Call-plan discipline. Deal reviews for field-completeness. We augment every MEDDPICC field with a PRISM pre-fill and an Exchange-earn: what the platform populates pre-call, and what the rep confirms through reciprocal dialog.

**Why is the new model better now, specifically because of PRISM and the AI-era buying context?** Three converging reasons. First, PRISM collapses the information gap the old discovery model was designed to close — making its central activity (fact-finding) obsolete and actively harmful. Second, AI-assisted buyers weight credibility and tradeoff honesty above all other signals (82% credibility over likability, Sales Growth 2025 🟡; 2.8× higher purchase ease from sensemaking suppliers, Gartner 🟢) — exactly what Exchange is designed to deliver. Third, the Algolia-specific competitive narrative ("developer tool vs. Constructor's merchandiser tool") cannot be neutralized by traditional discovery; it must be pre-empted in the first five minutes via named Exchange moves, and PRISM is the only mechanism that produces the buyer-specific data required to do so credibly.

**How does PRISM change the rules of what discovery is for?** Discovery is no longer about *learning what is true* (PRISM already surfaces most of it). Discovery is now about (a) *confirming what is true in the buyer's own words* — which is what actually fills MEDDPICC fields with commitment-grade language; (b) *earning the right to ask what PRISM cannot see* — internal politics, sequencing, emotional history, what the last failed project cost someone their credibility; (c) *sensemaking in the presence of overwhelming public information* — helping the buyer prioritize conflicting AI-surfaced signals. The rep's unit of value shifts from information-gathering to judgment, peer-match, and tradeoff honesty.

**What must be true for this methodology to succeed in the field?** Five preconditions, in descending order of importance: (1) **PRISM data quality must be rigorously QA'd before any hypothesis is delivered to a customer.** A confidently-framed wrong hypothesis in the first five minutes is near-unrecoverable. (2) **Reps must have ≥12 months category depth** or be paired with SEs who do — the load-bearing assumption of Exchange is rep quality, not PRISM quality. (3) **Enablement must measure "invitation-to-correct" behavior**, not just hypothesis delivery — reps under time pressure cut the invitation, and that collapses Exchange to a data-dressed pitch. (4) **Exceptions must be named and taught**: Exchange should not be deployed with hostile buyers, procurement-led regulated deals, early-career buyers who need to demonstrate process rigor, or when PRISM data confidence is low. (5) **Algolia should instrument its own A/B measurement** of Exchange vs. SPIN-control openings within its funnel to move the methodology's evidence base from 🔵 inference to 🟢 proven-for-Algolia — because no one has run this study yet in enterprise B2C commerce search, and the methodology should not rest permanently on transposition from adjacent disciplines.

**What is the foundational translation ruleset PRISM needs to implement?** The full specification is Section 6 above. Summarized: ten finding categories (SA, EC, TS, HS, FS, CA, BC, WB, TE, AC + proposed BL, RG, CM), eight output types (H, EM, QS, OP, CN, OE, NH, DT), seven metadata fields on every output (Confidence, Archetype-sensitivity, Persona-sensitivity, Evidence grounding, Risk-if-wrong, Evidence tier, Deploy flags), six sequencing rules with explicit branch logic for the four buyer-reaction classes (confirms/amends/rejects/deflects), and a guardrail-escalation to revert to SPIN when two hypotheses are rejected. Every compiled artifact is single-page, scannable, tagged with buyer archetype and persona, grounded in named evidence, and terminates in something a skimming rep can execute without rehearsal. No concept ships without (1) full explanation with evidence tier, (2) 10-second compressed version, (3) a concrete Algolia in-call example. This is buildable, not conceptual. Build against it.
